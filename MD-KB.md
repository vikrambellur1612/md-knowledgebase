Both WhatsApp groups run under the **Margdarshak** volunteer mentor community.

**What this document is:** A synthesis of real questions parents asked, real answers given by experienced admins/mentors (several identified themselves as having gone through this process in prior years — "Margdarshak 2023-24" etc.), real official notices/dates/links shared in the groups, and real cutoff/experience data points — both from parent-shared messages and from the standalone cutoff dataset. Nothing in this document has been invented — every fact, number, and quote is drawn from one of the three sources above. Opinions, anecdotes, and informal crowd-sourced data (as opposed to official rules or the machine-extracted cutoff figures) are explicitly labeled as such throughout.

**What this document is NOT:**
- Not an official rulebook. Official bodies (NTA, JoSAA, CSAB, BITS, IIIT-H/B, KEA, COMEDK, etc.) change rules, dates, fees, and quotas every year — **always verify current-year specifics against the official website before acting.**
- Not comprehensive on every exam. Even across all three sources, coverage of VITEEE, SRMJEEE, MET (Manipal), and Amrita (AEEE) remains comparatively thin (Section 8), because even the KCET/COMEDK group deliberately redirected those discussions to a further sister group.
- Not a source of exact repeatable dates or ranks. All specific 2025–26-cycle dates and cutoff figures below are **historical data points from one admission cycle**, useful to understand *sequencing and typical competitiveness*, not to be treated as this year's actual dates or ranks — this is especially true for KCET/COMEDK, whose schedules visibly shifted and got extended repeatedly within this single cycle alone, and whose Round 1 cutoffs (§7.12) typically loosen further in later rounds.
- The §7.12 cutoff dataset specifically is **AI-assisted extraction from official publications, not independently re-verified row by row** — treat every individual rank as needing a cross-check against KEA's/COMEDK's own official cutoff PDFs before being used for a real decision.

**How to use this document (for AI agents answering parent queries):** Treat this as grounded, citable background knowledge for questions about the Indian engineering admission process (JEE/BITS/IIIT/JoSAA/CSAB/KCET/COMEDK ecosystem), typical timelines, fee structures, counselling mechanics, cutoff competitiveness, and the kinds of practical/emotional issues parents commonly face. When a parent asks about *current-year* dates, cutoffs, or rules, direct them to verify on the relevant official website — this document tells you *what to expect and where to look*, not this year's exact number.

## 2. Master Admission-Cycle Timeline (as it actually unfolded, Sep 2025 – Aug 2026)

Use this as a **sequencing reference**, not as exact dates for a future year — dates shift annually, but the *order* and *approximate spacing* of events is a reliable pattern.

| Period | Milestone |
|---|---|
| Oct–Nov 2025 | JEE Main Session 1 registration opens (~3rd/4th week Oct), stays open ~1 month. Various private-exam application windows also open/close in this period (KLEEE, VITEEE, MET, SRMJEEE application windows spread Nov 2025–Apr 2026). |
| Nov 2025 | JEE Main Session 1 application closes (~27 Nov in this cycle) |
| Dec 2025 | BITSAT registration opens (~15 Dec); IIIT-H UGEE-related prep discussions begin |
| Jan 2026 | JEE Main Session 1 exam (~last week of Jan, spread across ~10 days/2 shifts a day); KCET application window opens, with its stated deadline extended at least twice (from ~15 Mar → 17 Feb → 22 Feb, illustrating how routinely KEA deadlines move) |
| Feb 2026 | JEE Main Session 1 result (~mid-Feb); IIIT-H UGEE registration opens; JEE Main Session 2 registration window; KCET school-level document verification window (~20 Feb per Info Bulletin) |
| Mar–Apr 2026 | JEE Main Session 2 exam (~first week of Apr); KCET written exam (~22–24 Apr in this cycle); BITSAT Session 1 (mid-Apr in this cycle) |
| Apr 2026 | JEE Main final combined result + AIR + JEE Advanced eligibility declared (~20 Apr); JEE Advanced registration opens for qualified candidates (~23 Apr); KCET provisional answer key (~25 Apr) |
| May 2026 | JEE Advanced exam (~17 May); BITSAT Session 2 (~24–27 May); IIIT-H UGEE results; IIIT-B application window; KCET/board marks-entry window opens; KCET result targeted for last week of May but commonly slips into June (2026: pushed past 1–2 June due to Agriculture-quota practical-exam delays) |
| Jun 2026 | JEE Advanced result (~1 Jun); JoSAA registration opens (day after JEE Advanced result) → Mock allotments → Round 1 (~10 days after registration opens); BITS FD Preference Form due (~1 Jun, later extended); BITS Iteration 1 (~15–20 Jun); KCET/COMEDK seat matrix released as "draft" then "final" (2026: real delay here pushed the whole KCET counselling schedule back); KCET option/choice entry opens (2026: stated deadline genuinely disputed across sources — 26 vs 30 June — eventually settled around 30 June) |
| Jun–Jul 2026 | JoSAA Rounds 1–5 (Round 1 ~2 weeks due to heavy document verification; later rounds ~4–6 days each); IIIT-B, IIIT-H JEE-mode, JAC Delhi Round-1 results roll in alongside JoSAA; KCET Mock Seat Allotment (~6–9 Jul in this cycle); COMEDK counselling registration (~8–10 Jun) and Mock Round choice filling (~11–13 Jul) |
| Late Jul 2026 | JoSAA Round 5 ends (~21 Jul); IIT institute reporting begins (~20 Jul onward, institute-specific dates); PAF payment window for NIT+ System retention (~22–25 Jul); BITS final iterations, BITS campus reporting (~28–30 Jul); COMEDK Round 1 choice-filling deadline (repeatedly extended, ~20→24→25 Jul), Round 1 allotment/decision/fee-payment window (~28–31 Jul); AICTE's own deadline (~20 Jul) creates real forfeiture risk for families holding a paid seat elsewhere while awaiting KCET/COMEDK |
| Aug 2026 | CSAB registration and rounds (early Aug–mid Aug); NIT+ System institute reporting (~13–18 Aug onward); KCET Round 1 result (2026: ~16 Aug, notably late that cycle, partly to align with NEET/medical counselling timing); PESU-KCET (PES's own separate counselling) starts (~16 Aug); KCET Round 2 and COMEDK later rounds continue into late Aug; group winds down as admissions conclude |

**Key structural takeaway:** Nearly all major counselling processes (BITS Iterations, JoSAA, IIIT-B, IIIT-H, JAC Delhi, KCET, COMEDK) run **concurrently** in June–August, not sequentially — families frequently had to make accept/float/freeze decisions on one seat *before* knowing their outcome elsewhere (BITS vs JoSAA; a JEE-based PES seat vs a hoped-for KCET seat; a paid AICTE-college seat vs a delayed KCET result). Plan for this overlap; it was a major source of stress and second-guessing throughout both transcripts. **KCET/COMEDK's own internal schedule was also unusually volatile within this single cycle** — deadlines were extended repeatedly, the KCET result was pushed past its usual late-May target into June due to Agriculture-quota-exam delays, and the government's own seat-matrix approval visibly delayed the start of counselling — treat every KCET/COMEDK date above as especially provisional.

## 3. JEE Main

### 3.1 Structure
- Two sessions/attempts a year: **Session 1** (~last week Jan) and **Session 2** (~first week Apr). The **best of the two "Total" NTA-score percentiles** counts toward the final All India Rank (AIR), which is announced only after Session 2.
- Conducted by NTA; portal **jeemain.nta.nic.in**. Roughly 10 exam days per session, 2 shifts/day.
- Each shift gets the same question set (order/option-order randomized per candidate); each shift is normalized **independently** into a percentile, so raw marks are not comparable across shifts — only percentile is.
- NTA releases **percentile only** (up to 7 decimal places), never raw subject marks, for both sessions individually and the Final NTA Score.
- No on-screen calculator is provided (an early brochure draft briefly suggested otherwise; NTA clarified — no calculator).
- 2026-cycle news report claimed exam-centre allocation would shift to being tied to the candidate's **Aadhaar-card address** rather than a free city-preference choice starting with the 2026–27 session — flagged by admins as a **news article, not (yet) an official NTA circular** at the time it was shared; always verify against nta.ac.in / jeemain.nta.nic.in directly.
- New requirement introduced in the 2026 cycle: candidates had to supply their **Class 11 board registration number** at JEE Main registration (NTA's stated reason: to prevent exam-date clashes with board practicals/exams).

### 3.2 Percentile → Rank mechanics (a well-explained, recurring topic)
- Percentile is a **normalized ordinal position within your own shift's cohort**, not a measure of raw marks. Each shift has at least one candidate at 100 percentile; their absolute marks vary shift to shift but are all treated as equal.
- Illustrative real data point: Session 1 (01 Feb 2024), Shift 1, 121,205 candidates — a total of 109 marks (a very tough shift) translated to P 97.73 / C 93.92 / M 24.23, Total/NTA Score 95.37 percentile. Meanwhile ~170+ marks in an easier shift that year may have given 99+ percentile. This is the core reason "how many marks did you get" is a meaningless comparison across shifts/dates.
- Number of candidates who got NTA Score = 100 (both sessions combined, Paper 1): 56 in 2024, 24 in 2025.
- **Tie-break order** for identical percentiles: (1) higher Maths percentile → (2) higher Physics percentile → (3) higher Chemistry percentile → (4) lower ratio of incorrect:correct answers → (5) age (historically used, being phased out/de-emphasized).
- A widely-used **planning heuristic** shared by admins: assume up to ~50% "churn" in the top ranks between Session 1 and the final (April) result, when trying to estimate where a Session-1-only percentile might eventually land in AIR terms. This is an approximation for planning purposes, not a formula NTA publishes.
- Final AIR is computed across the **full unique-candidate pool of both sessions combined**, not scaled by each session's individual candidate count.

**Important distinction repeatedly clarified in-group:** *Qualifying* for JEE Advanced (clearing the cutoff above) is very different from being *competitive* for an IIT seat of real interest. Admins stressed that in the General category, landing within roughly the **top ~20,000 JEE Advanced ranks** is closer to the threshold for a meaningful IIT/branch outcome — and tighter still if the family is set on a specific IIT or branch (circuit branches at top IITs closed within roughly the first ~150 ranks in the 2026 cycle, per one parent's Round-1-adjacent report — treat as one data point).
- Roughly 2.5 lakh candidates (all categories) become eligible to attempt JEE Advanced each year; in practice ~1.8 lakh actually sit the exam (2024, 2025); of these, roughly 54,000 are ultimately given a JEE Advanced rank (i.e., cross the ~35% aggregate CRL-inclusion threshold — see §4).

### 3.4 JoSAA general eligibility rule (12th-marks criterion — applies to NITs/IIITs/IITs/GFTIs)
Must satisfy **at least ONE** of:
1. **≥75% aggregate** in Class XII (or equivalent) board (SC/ST/PwD relaxed to **≥65%**), OR
2. Within the **category-wise top 20 percentile** of successful candidates in one's own board (published at josaa.nic.in/top-20-percentile/).

There is **NO individual subject-wise (PCM) minimum-marks requirement** for JoSAA/JEE Main eligibility — a very commonly-worried-about myth that was repeatedly and explicitly debunked in the group. (For boards that don't publish/calculate their own top-20-percentile cutoff, or an unlisted board, JoSAA falls back to using the CBSE category-wise cutoff as the benchmark — confirmed via a real case of a Karnataka PUC student.)

**BITS eligibility is different and stricter:** minimum aggregate **75% in PCM/B subjects, with at least 60% in EACH individual subject.**

### 3.5 Required documents for JEE Main registration
- Passport photo (JPG, 10–200 KB, white/cream background, 3.5×4.5 cm)
- Signature (JPG, 4–30 KB, 3.5×1.5 cm, black pen, scanned)
- ID proof: Aadhaar preferred (else PAN / school ID / voter ID / passport / bank passbook / driving licence)
- Category certificate if claiming EWS/OBC-NCL/SC/ST — must be dated **on/after 1 April** of the admission year
- 10th marksheet
- 12th marksheet (only if dropper/repeater; freshers submit this later, at counselling)
- Gap certificate (droppers only)
- Migration certificate (if board/state/school changed)
- 12th passing certificate (needed later, at counselling — not at registration)
- Class 11 board registration number (new 2026-cycle requirement)

### 3.6 Exam-day essentials (admit card / exam centre)
- Carry: transparent water bottle, simple transparent ball-point pen, colour A4 printout of the admit card (with Self-Declaration/Undertaking filled — photo pasted, left thumb impression), 1 extra passport photo for the attendance sheet, original photo ID matching what was declared.
- Aadhaar biometrics should be pre-unlocked (mAadhaar app or myaadhaar.uidai.gov.in) before exam day; carry the original PVC/e-Aadhaar (colour printout, digital-signature tick visible).
- If NTA flagged an Aadhaar-photo/live-photo mismatch, also carry the signed, school-principal-attested identity verification certificate.
- A bio-break mid-exam triggers re-frisking + re-biometric verification (time lost) — plan water intake sensibly.
- The **city intimation slip** is released before the admit card (confirms city only); the exact date/shift/centre appears only on the final admit card — and these have, in real cases, **changed between the two documents**, so always trust the final admit card and re-download it close to the exam date.
- Standard "don't carry" list applies (see §9 Logistics for the full checklist, common to JEE Main/BITSAT/JEE Advanced).

### 3.7 Answer key / result process (2026-cycle example)
- Provisional answer key released a few days after each exam session; **challenge window** open ~4–6 days, fee **₹200 per question** (non-refundable).
- Final answer key + Session result: roughly 2–3 weeks after the exam.
- Final combined result (after Session 2): ~20 April in the 2026 cycle, showing Session 1, Session 2, Final NTA Score, AIR, and category-wise JEE Advanced eligibility (ELIGIBLE / NOT ELIGIBLE).
- JEE Advanced registration for qualified candidates opened ~23 April (10 AM) in this cycle.
- Scorecard also downloadable via **DigiLocker**.

### 3.8 Informal prep/score-checking tools mentioned (not endorsements)
NTA Abhyas app / nta.ac.in/Quiz (official, old papers only); third-party score calculators/analysis referenced informally by parents: Aakash JEE Main Score Calculator, cracku.in, Mathongo (score calculator + shift-difficulty analysis videos, useful ~1–2 days after the answer key drops).

## 4. JEE Advanced

### 4.1 Structure & 2026-cycle key facts
- Held on a single day, two papers (Paper 1 & Paper 2). 2026 exam date: **17 May**. Official dates page: **jeeadv.ac.in/imp_dates.html**.
- Candidates list **10 city/venue choices** at registration; centre allocation is random (not tied to form-fill timing). JEE Advanced has far fewer centres than JEE Main, so odds of a nearby centre were described by an admin as better, not worse.
- 2026 admit card portal: cprofile26.jeeadv.ac.in. Reporting time on the admit card: 7:00 AM.
- There is a minimum **~35% aggregate marks** threshold for inclusion in the Common Rank List (CRL) — a long-standing rule, not new. Category-wise inclusion criteria can shift year to year depending on how the exam played out (2023 was cited as an especially tough year; 2025's criteria were even lower than 2023's).
- Only JEE Advanced rank-holders can list IITs in their JoSAA choice list; everyone with a valid JEE Main rank can list the full NIT+ System spread regardless.

### 4.2 Results/toppers by year (illustrative reference points, not predictive of future cutoffs)
- **2024:** AIR 1 Ved Lahoti, 355/360 (Maths 117, Phy 118, Chem 120) — called one of the easiest papers in 10 years; ~100th rank ≈ 300 marks.
- **2025:** AIR 1 Rajit Gupta, 332/360 (Maths 111, Phy 116, Chem 105); top female candidate Devdutta Majhi, 312/360, AIR 16 overall. ~100th rank ≈ 270 marks (tougher than 2024).
- **2026:** AIR 1 Shubham Kumar, 330/360; AIR 2 Kabeer Chhillar, 329; AIR 3 Jatin Chahar, 319 (all IIT Delhi zone). Top female candidate Arohi Deshpande (IIT Delhi zone), 280/360, overall CRL rank 77.
- Score-to-rank crowd-sourced distributions (Mathongo, ~30K+ self-reported samples; also an official IIT Kanpur-published distribution) were used informally by parents while awaiting results.

### 4.3 Answer key / result timeline (2026 example)
Provisional answer keys (Paper 1 & 2) released ~8 days after the exam; challenge window followed with **no separate challenge fee** (unlike JEE Main). Result declared **1 June 2026** via cdata.jeeadv.ac.in/result2026/ (enter Roll Number). "Criteria for inclusion in rank list" published as a dedicated PDF each year on jeeadv.ac.in.

### 4.4 Notable IIT/branch context surfaced in parent discussions (qualitative — clearly opinions/anecdotes, not rankings)
- Among the original 7 IITs, only Kharagpur, Guwahati, and Roorkee have "Electronics" literally in a branch name; elsewhere (e.g., IIT Bombay, IIT Madras) electronics content sits inside the Electrical Engineering department — students pick relevant electives (Analog/Digital Design, etc.).
- **IIT Madras "IDDD"** (inter-disciplinary dual degree): students can opt in around 3rd year for added specialization flexibility, and can drop out of it later if desired.
- **IIT Madras online BS degrees** (non-JEE track: Data Science and Applications, Electronic Systems, Aeronautics and Space Technology, Management and Data Science) — admission via a Qualifier exam after 4 weeks of free coursework; JEE-Advanced-eligible candidates get direct entry to Foundation level. Genuine (not a scam, despite the "you've been selected" marketing emails causing suspicion in the group). Some students who didn't get their first-choice regular college take this up as a supplement, but many discontinue once their main college's year-2/3 workload increases.
- **IISc Bangalore B.Tech**: previously only Mathematics & Computing (~40 seats + ~8 women's-reservation + ~4 NRI/foreign ≈ ~52). 2026 added 3 new B.Tech branches — Materials Science & Engineering, Mechanics and Computing, Aerospace Engineering (similar seat pattern). Eligibility is **60% in 12th** (lower than JoSAA's usual 75%), even though IISc B.Tech is now inside JoSAA. Admission: B.Tech via JEE Advanced only (+small NRI/foreign quota); BS programs (~138–140 seats) via JEE Advanced or the IISER Aptitude Test (IAT) — open-category historical BS cutoff ~AIR 2,500–3,000 (much tighter for B.Tech Maths & Computing). Foreign nationals can now use SAT scores (~1400–1450+ recommended) instead of JEE Advanced, plus an interview.
- **IIT Delhi–Abu Dhabi**: ran a multi-city 2026 recruitment roadshow (Pune, Chennai, Mumbai, Kochi, Calicut, Kolkata, Delhi, Hyderabad, Bengaluru) for JEE Advanced candidates; register via iitdabudhabi.ac.ae/jee-admissions.
- Recurring, purely-anecdotal brand/placement commentary from parents/admins (present both sides, no consensus claimed): IIT Bombay/Delhi/Madras remain considered top-tier; newer IITs (Hyderabad, Indore, Gandhinagar, etc.) are "improving fast" but not yet matching old-IIT brand weight; IIT Hyderabad specifically flagged as progressing quickest among newer IITs, with a locational Biotech advantage (Hyderabad = pharma/biotech hub); IIT Delhi seen as more "corporate/placement/entrepreneurship" oriented, IISc Bangalore as more "research/deep-tech" oriented — both considered elite, personal-fit decision; IIT Kanpur seen as strong in theoretical CS with a large alumni base, IIT Delhi as more industry-oriented; BITS Pilani/Goa/Hyderabad and IIIT-H/B are generally bucketed by the group in the same broad tier as IIT/NIT, "BITS = private IIT" being a common shorthand; a shared news article claimed Civil Engineering saw a preference surge at IITs in the 2026 cycle "amid AI and infrastructure development" trends — presented as market commentary, not verified fact.
- **BTech Biotechnology outlook** (from a doctor-parent in the group, one professional's perspective): non-IIT BTech Biotech ≈ ₹4–6 LPA starting (mostly ops/manufacturing/projects roles); non-IIT MTech Biotech ≈ ₹8–11 LPA; IIT-tag MTech up to ≈ ₹15L. Advice given: BTech alone is only an introduction to the field — plan the MTech/PhD specialization (bioprocess development, bioengineering, fermentation tech, cell culture, etc.) deliberately, since biotech graduate supply outpaces current industry hiring capacity.
- **GATE** was repeatedly named as the standard next step in India for MTech/research after any UG institute — illustrated with a counter-example of Amrita Vishwa Vidyapeetham (non-IIT/NIT) students achieving AIR 1 and AIR 6 in GATE 2025 Aerospace, to make the point that "lower-tier" UG doesn't preclude strong outcomes if the student stays genuinely engaged.

## 5. BITSAT & BITS Pilani / Goa / Hyderabad

### 5.1 Campuses & positioning
- Three India campuses: **Pilani** (est. 1969, original/main), **Goa** (est. 2004), **Hyderabad** (est. 2008); also BITS Dubai (international).
- Historically Pilani was most preferred; in recent years Goa and Hyderabad are increasingly preferred (especially by South Indian families) for proximity and reputation as strong "coding hubs." BITS Goa was cited in-group as ranked #9 in an unspecified national ranking.
- Admin comparison: academics/placements are "almost the same" across all three campuses; alumni base is strongest at Pilani (oldest); management does not officially differentiate between campuses. A real cutoff-gap data point: ~25–30 marks delta observed between Pilani and other campuses for the same branch (e.g., ECE), Pilani running higher.
- Airport-distance context that shaped many families' campus preference: Goa's Dabolim airport ~5 km from campus; Hyderabad ~23 km; Pilani 4–6 hrs by road to Jaipur/Delhi.
- Anecdotal, second-hand (unverified) student feedback: BITS Goa's grading is comparatively tougher ("7.5 CGPA in Goa ≈ 8.5 in Hyderabad" per some students) with a perceived ~8.0 CGPA minimum for some campus placements — presented explicitly as student-sourced impression, not confirmed institutional fact. A separate, directly-conflicting parent report described BITS overall as academically heavy with a "just-do-it" culture and insufficient support for student mental-health pressure; this was immediately and directly disputed by another admin whose own child attends BITS, who described active peer-support systems, a 24/7 helpline, and flexible/lighter elective options (e.g., Bhagavad Gita, photography, movie-making, Carnatic music courses). Both perspectives are preserved here as genuinely conflicting parent accounts.

### 5.2 Eligibility
- Minimum **75% aggregate in PCM/B**, with **at least 60% in EACH individual subject** (stricter than JoSAA's blanket-75% rule).
- Age/DOB cutoffs can be stricter than other exams in a given cycle — in 2026, BITSAT's application only accepted candidates born up to June 2008, which unexpectedly blocked at least one Dec-2008-born candidate who had been accepted under the same DOB by JEE Main and their PU board — **always check the current year's DOB cutoff specifically for BITSAT, don't assume it matches JEE/board rules.**
- 12th-standard result generally needs to be available by 1 June for BITS eligibility documentation. NIOS students have historically been accommodated case-by-case, treated on par with CBSE.

### 5.3 Exam format
- 100% computer-based; **every candidate gets a different, randomly-generated paper** from a large question bank, even within the same shift — BITS never publishes past papers, response sheets, or answer keys, so **nothing can be "challenged"** after the exam (a fundamental difference from JEE).
- Bonus mechanism: finishing all questions early can unlock 15 additional bonus questions.
- **Part IV choice: Maths OR Biology** (Physics & Chemistry compulsory for all). Choosing Maths keeps all BITS courses open; Biology restricts mainly to B.Pharm.
- Score shown **on-screen immediately** after the exam; the official scorecard PDF follows ~3–4 days later and occasionally differs from the on-screen score ("Moderated BITSAT score" — BITS's stated explanation is correction for verified erroneous questions, not itemized publicly). **No BITSAT rank or percentile is ever published** — cutoffs are quoted purely in raw-score terms, and BITSAT does **not** normalize/percentile scores across shifts the way JEE does (it uses marks largely as-is, adjusted only for verified question errors).
- CBSE candidates should select **"AISSCE CBAT"** from the board dropdown (a recurring point of confusion — not "Any other board").

### 5.4 2026-cycle operational problems (a strong "what to watch for" list for future cycles)
- **City/slot allocation was widely NOT honored** — many Bangalore-area families were routed to distant cities (Hubballi, Shimoga, Mysuru) despite stating Bangalore-area preferences; BITS's official stance was that the allotted city is **final, with no changes accommodated under any circumstance.** This triggered a public petition and heavy complaint volume; compared unfavorably by parents to JEE Main's larger-scale, better centre-matching process.
- **Admit cards were initially issued with wrong dates/slots**, later withdrawn and reissued/corrected — advise re-downloading the latest admit card close to the exam and re-verifying date/time/centre before any non-refundable travel booking.
- **Unexplained score-reduction complaints** for specific shifts (esp. 16 April Session-2 Shift 2) — score drops >10 marks between on-screen and final scorecard were reported by multiple families with no clear public explanation, a recurring transparency complaint.
- Useful escalation contact used by parents: **dean.dasfa@bits-pilani.ac.in** (reported responsive), alongside general **bitsat@pilani.bits-pilani.ac.in**. Official 2026 BITSAT helpdesk: **+91 22610 87545**; also 01596-255294 / 255330 / 255540 / 255541; **admn.payment@pilani.bits-pilani.ac.in** for payment-specific queries; hours Mon–Fri 9–1 & 2–5, Sat 9–1.
- 2026 Session-2 dates were shifted to avoid a Sunday: Mon 25 / Tue 26 / Wed 27 May.

### 5.5 Fees, counselling ("Iterations"), and Preference Form
- BITSAT exam fee separate from the **BITS FD (First Degree) Preference Form fee (~₹1,000)** — effectively the "BITS Counselling Fee," paid to enter BITS's own counselling after getting a BITSAT score.
- 2026 Preference Form last date: 1 June (fillable after 12th results and after BITSAT Session 2; later extended when the edit window itself was delayed).
- BITS counselling rounds are called **"Iterations."** 2026 had roughly 4–5 iterations, reporting to campus ~28–30 July.
- **Waitlist mechanics:** a fee (₹64,100 cited by one parent — bracket-specific, confirm current figure) keeps a candidate in the iteration pool. Three outcomes: (1) still waitlisted after the final iteration → full refund; (2) offered a seat, choose to freeze or float for better in later iterations → pay balance and join; (3) offered a seat you don't want, decline → forfeit amount already paid (no separate action needed).
- **Freeze/Slide options** exist after accepting a seat and paying (similar spirit to JoSAA's Freeze/Float/Slide), though UI labels caused some confusion — verify current-cycle wording directly on the portal.
- **Withdrawal** happens via a dedicated portal link (2026: bitspilaniedu.com/FD2026/Bits/Default.aspx); refund/deduction depends on the iteration/stage of withdrawal (earlier = less deduction) — confirm current terms in the offer letter, as parents found BITS's own communication on this point unclear/inconsistent.
- **2026 new scheme — "Tuition-Blind Admissions":** BITSAT **top 500 rankers** with annual family income up to **₹20 Lakh** get a **100% tuition fee waiver for all 4 years**, conditional on maintaining **7.5 CGPA**. This appears to be a **one-time offer at admission**, not automatically cascaded to rank 501+ if a top-500 ranker declines (parents' working understanding, not explicitly BITS-confirmed in-thread). This scheme, plus BITS's preference-deadline falling *before* JoSAA results were known, was cited by admins as together driving an unusually **high** Iteration-1 cutoff in 2026.
- Older, pre-existing **Merit** and **Merit-cum-Means (MCN)** scholarships also exist (not new): everyone pays full tuition upfront; eligible students apply separately and get reimbursed ~1–2 months later; merit is allotted first, then merit-cum-means, whichever benefits the student more; unclaimed scholarship is not reallocated.
- **Fee snapshot (2026, BITS Pilani site):** ~₹32+ lakh total for a 4-year single degree; ~₹40+ lakh for a 5-year dual degree (tuition only, excludes hostel). Separate **"Summer Term" fee (~₹1 lakh)** covers the mandatory 8-week PS1 internship, and/or optional remedial summer courses for students with weak grades.
- **BITS 2+2 international**, **3+2 (BE+MBA)**, and the **"Five-Year Intercampus Engineering and Management Programme"** run on **separate application/iteration tracks** from the regular BE/MSc FD process — each has its own academic-calendar PDF and iteration dates.
- Original documents are typically only **verified** (not retained) on campus-reporting day; joining-formalities email arrives after fee payment with the full document checklist.

### 5.6 Dual-Degree (MSc + BE, 5-year) mechanics — a very frequently-asked topic
- Dual-degree candidates are admitted, at BITSAT-score time, **only into an MSc branch** (Chemistry/Physics/Maths/Biology/Economics, etc.). The BE (engineering) branch is decided **after 1st year**, based on 1st-year CGPA + student preference — **it is not guaranteed or chosen at initial admission.**
- Roughly **25–30% of a given BE branch's seats** (e.g., ~100 of ~200 CSE seats, per an admin's illustrative estimate) are reserved for 2nd-year Dual-degree entrants; competition for a specific BE branch (e.g., CSE) in year 2 is **only among the Dual-degree/MSc pool**, not against direct-BE admits.
- Course timeline: Year 1 = common subjects; Year 2 = MSc subjects only; Year 3 = BE subjects only (with the junior BE batch); Year 4 = 50% MSc + 50% BE; Year 5 = MSc thesis + BE project/thesis.
- Popular/"achievable" combos: **Economics + CSE**, or **Physics + CSE/ECE/EEE/ENI**. Dual-degree students are called "Dulas"/"Dualites" in BITS slang and sit together with direct-BE candidates for placements (no separate track).
- Fintech/industry-preferred combos noted: MSc Economics + BE CSE (or BE CSE/Maths&Computing + Economics minor); MSc Biology + BE CSE for genetics/AI research; MSc Physics + BE ECE/EEE/E&I for semiconductor research/further study.
- Trade-off vs. a straight 4-year BE elsewhere (e.g., an IIT): Dual-degree = 5 years, branch uncertain until after year 1, higher fees, +2 years if pursuing further studies. Straight BE = definite branch from day 1, shorter, generally lower fees. Admins call the dual-degree path "hectic, especially years 3–4," but say "most students do it and come out successfully" — should be chosen based on genuine interest in the specific MSc subject, not just as a backdoor to a BE brand.
- Extensive elective flexibility from ~2nd year onward: core department courses + department electives + open electives from **any** department.

### 5.7 Internships & placements (BITS-specific terminology)
- **PS1** (Practice School 1): mandatory 2-month internship after 2nd year, 5 credits, allotted by CGPA + preference (often near hometown).
- **PS2** (Practice School 2): mandatory ~6-month final-year internship (7th OR 8th sem, student's choice), can be a company placement or a Thesis at a university/research institute in India or abroad; 20 credits, mandatory part of the course.
- **SIP** (Summer Internship Program, after 3rd year): available only to students taking PS2 in 8th sem; optional, not credited; company test + tech + HR interview process similar to full placement; <15% selection rate; can lead to a Pre-Placement Offer (PPO); good CV addition even without selection.
- Placement support (3rd year, 6th sem): company talks, mock tests, alumni-run mock interviews, optional 2–3 week paid summer boot camp.
- **Placement-statistics interpretation caution (admin's guidance, broadly applicable to ANY college, not just BITS):** (1) "% placed" is calculated only among students who were both eligible AND opted in — not the full batch; (2) relative grading + a CGPA cutoff for placement eligibility further shrinks that pool; (3) "average package" is typically inflated by a few high IT-sector offers — always check average **vs. median**, and check **core-branch-specific** placement numbers separately, since many core/non-CS students end up taking IT jobs anyway.
- General market colour: 2025–26-cycle campus placements were reported tougher for freshers industry-wide — not BITS-specific — hitting local/Tier-2-and-below colleges hardest.
- Illustrative (non-representative) anecdote: a BITS Mechanical Engineering student (good but not topper CGPA) chose a 7th-sem Thesis over PS2, got BITS faculty recommendation letters, and secured a funded 6-month research placement at Georgia Tech (accommodation provided; family paid only airfare/visa).

### 5.8 Cutoffs/scores (indicative — always cross-check against BITS's own official cutoff page)
- **Official source:** admissions.bits-pilani.ac.in/FD/BITSAT_cutOffs.html.
- 2026 Session 1 was widely called "one of the toughest shifts ever"; informal parent/coaching estimates floated mid-season: CS Pilani cutoff could fall as low as ~250; anything above ~200 was called "excellent," 150+ seen by some as "surely enough for BITS" — **these are mid-season informal estimates, not official cutoffs.**

A BITS Goa CSE candidate's 4th-preference outcome landed at 316 in Iteration 1 (missed Pilani CSE/MnC/ECE) — illustrating that even solid scores can miss top choices early, with Iteration 2+ often bringing upgrades.

## 6. IIITs (Indian & International Institutes of Information Technology)

### 6.1 The landscape
- ~26–29 total IIITs nationally (count varies by year as new ones open). Naming convention: **IIIT-Hyderabad and IIIT-Bangalore are "International"** Institutes of Information Technology (private, NOT government-run despite the name); **IIIT-Delhi is "Indraprastha"** (a Delhi State University); **all other ~24 IIITs are "Indian"** Institutes of Information Technology (mostly centrally-funded or public-private-funded, admitted via JEE Main + JoSAA/CSAB).
- IIITs at Naya Raipur and Bhubaneswar are also "International" IIITs but ARE covered under JoSAA/CSAB (listed under GFTIs).
- At regular (non-H/B/D) IIITs, branches are essentially limited to **CSE, ECE (and variants), or IT** — EEE exists only at IIIT Gwalior. IIITDM-named institutes (Design & Manufacturing) offer a few additional branches.
- **Top-4 IIITs under JoSAA/CSAB**, repeatedly named by admins as the strongest of the "regular" IIITs: **Allahabad, Lucknow, Gwalior, Jabalpur.**
- IIIT-H and IIIT-B were both established in 1998; IIIT-D was established in 2008 as a Delhi State Technical University.

### 6.2 IIIT-Hyderabad (IIIT-H) — private, multiple direct-application admission modes
IIIT-H has (per the group) **7 different UG admission modes**, each requiring a **separate application** (ugadmissions.iiit.ac.in):
1. **JEE (Main) mode** — for single-degree 4-year B.Tech: **CSE 100 seats, ECE 70 seats**; no interview; 25% seats reserved for female candidates. JEE Main cutoff for this mode "has not breached 99 percentile in the last 6 years except in 2022," per an admin's tracked history. Applications open ~mid-April; last date around mid-May (verify each year).
2. **UGEE (own entrance exam) mode** — for **5-year dual-degree programs only** (B.Tech + MSc with research). Seats per program: CSD 30, ECD 20, CND 15, CLD 15, CHD 15, CGD 15 (total ~110), +25% female reservation. Registration opens ~Feb, exam ~3rd week of April.
   - **UGEE exam format:** two components in one 180-minute session — **SUPR** (Subject Proficiency Test, 60 min, PCM at JEE-Main/Class-11-12 level) and **REAP** (Research Aptitude Test, 120 min, logical reasoning/analytical/data-interpretation, unique to UGEE and NOT standard PCM content). MCQs, ~25% negative marking in both sections. Both sections must be cleared independently; SUPR often acts as a first filter.
   - **After the exam:** shortlisting on SUPR+REAP scores → interview (often online) assessing problem-solving approach, research curiosity, communication clarity — interview performance significantly affects final ranking.
   - 2026 example cutoffs shared in-group: SUPR cutoff 19, REAP cutoff 67 (illustrative for that cycle only — cutoffs differ by gender since 25% of seats are female-reserved).
3. **SPEC mode** (newer, single-degree): CSE 10, ECE 10 seats.
4. **DASA mode** (mainly for OCI/foreign candidates): CSE 25, ECE 10 single-degree, +6 total dual-degree seats; considers SAT scores; fees higher than the standard track.
5. **Olympiad mode**: also open, exact CSE/ECE seat split not clearly known in-group.
6. **Board-topper mode**
7. **LEEE mode**: for lateral entry (someone at another college for 2 years wanting to switch to IIIT-H) — noted as unlikely to be relevant for most fresh applicants.
- **IIIT-H has 25% seats reserved for a "Diversity pool"** across both UGEE and JEE modes (in addition to, and distinct from, the female-only reservation).
- IIIT-H JEE-mode Round-1 seat allotment in a recent prior year happened around 12 June (reference point, not a fixed annual date).

### 6.3 IIIT-Bangalore (IIIT-B) — private, single admission mode
- B.Tech programs only started in 2024–25 (previously only 5-year Integrated M.Tech was offered).
- **Only one admission mode**: direct application using JEE Main / JEE Advanced ranks — **no separate entrance exam, no CBSE marks, no Olympiad consideration.**
- **2025-26 admissions introduced "BJR" (Best JEE Rank):** if a candidate has only a JEE Main rank, that's used; if they have BOTH JEE Main and JEE Advanced ranks, the **numerically lower (better) of the two** is used as their BJR — **no special weight is given to Advanced over Main**, it's a pure numeric comparison. Worked example: Candidate A (Main 9K, no Advanced) vs. Candidate B (Main 10K, Advanced 7K) → B's BJR is 7K, so **B ranks above A** despite A's better Main rank. The published cutoff column is simply labeled "Best JEE Rank Cutoff," without indicating whether an individual's number came from Main or Advanced.
- Registration typically opens ~first week of May; **2026 last date for applying + paying fee: 8 June.**
- Practical cutoff reality: "no cutoff to apply," but even a 99 percentile JEE Main score has been described as falling short in practice — **IIIT-B closed around a Best-JEE-Rank of ~9,300** in a recent year (reference point).
- **90 additional seats** were added at IIIT-B for the 2026 cycle (per an admin's note), potentially affecting future-year cutoffs.

### 6.4 IIIT-Delhi (IIIT-D) — Delhi State University, via JAC Delhi
- Admitted through **JAC Delhi counselling** (which also covers DTU and NSUT), **not JoSAA.**
- **85% of seats reserved for Delhi candidates; only 15% for Outside-Delhi candidates** (across ALL categories combined) — meaning General-category Outside-Delhi candidates compete for a very small slice (illustrative real numbers: Total Outside-Delhi seats 86, of which General/Gender-Neutral = 35, for one branch/year — treat as illustrative, not a fixed constant).
- Has two additional admission modes for two specific branches (Computer Science and Design; Computer Science and Social Sciences) — but these are **Delhi-candidates-only.**
- JAC Delhi registration typically opens ~last week of May.
- IIIT-D allotment uses an internal "IIIT-D rank" calculated after bonus-point inclusions (an unofficial rank-converter tool — iiitd-rank-converter.vercel.app — was built informally by a parent's child "for fun," explicitly flagged as **not to be trusted for accuracy**).

### 6.5 All other IIITs (~24, the "regular"/government-linked ones)
- Admitted purely via **JEE Main score/rank through JoSAA/CSAB** — **no separate application needed**, just select them in the standard JoSAA choice list. **JEE Advanced is NOT used** for these.
- No State quota (100% All-India merit, unlike NITs' 50/50 HS/OS split).
- Fee range (indicative, excludes hostel): IIIT-H/B/D ≈ ₹20–25 lakh total; other IIITs ≈ ₹10–15 lakh (some lower), depending on centrally-funded vs. public-private-funded status.

### 6.6 IIIT vs NIT vs BITS — recurring comparison framework from admins
Key factors admins consistently pointed families toward (not a fixed ranking, deliberately so):
- **Fees**; **Campus size/facilities**; **Government/PPP vs. Private status**; **long-term national/international brand recognition**; **Crowd mix** (IIITs = 100% All-India merit; Bangalore-area private colleges draw via ComedK + Management Quota, also nominally open All-India but check actual out-of-state %); **Hostel vs. Home** (most Bangalore-area colleges give hostel access mainly to non-local students).
- "Tier 2 NIT vs Tier 2 IIIT" is explicitly called a subjective, overused framing once you have 15+ of each — admins recommend building **your own priority buckets** by branch/location/cost fit rather than assuming a blanket ordering.
- IIITs (besides H/B) skew newer and IT/software-focused; NITs have a "wider canvas" of branches and longer track record. NIT tag is anecdotally (unconfirmed) considered better-regarded for Masters-abroad admissions; NITs also draw government/PSU recruiters at placements that IIITs/BITS typically don't get.

## 7. KCET & COMEDK (Karnataka state exams) — deep coverage

**Source note:** this section draws on a SECOND, dedicated Margdarshak transcript ("Marg 25-26 Engg - KCET/COMEDK," the sister group to the main JEE/BITS/IIIT one, also active Sep 2025–Aug 2026, ~10,600 messages). Unlike Section 8 below, KCET and COMEDK are covered here in real depth — this is genuinely one of the richest, most detailed parts of this whole knowledge base.

### 7.1 The core KCET vs COMEDK distinction

| | **KCET** | **COMEDK** |
|---|---|---|
| Run by | Karnataka Examinations Authority (KEA), Govt of Karnataka | A private-colleges consortium (not government) |
| Who can apply | **Minimum 7 years of study in Karnataka** (class 1–12), including at least one board exam (10th or 12th) completed in Karnataka | **All-India** — no domicile/residency requirement at all |
| Institutes covered | Government AND private institutes | **Private institutes only** — and not every KCET-linked private college participates in COMEDK (e.g., UVCE, PESU are KCET-only) |
| Seat share at a private college | ~45% of seats | ~30–35% of seats (Management Quota takes the rest, roughly 25%) |
| Reservation system | Full category-reservation system (SC/ST/OBC/EWS/rural/Kannada-medium/religious-minority/etc.) | **No caste-based reservation** — only a minor Kalyana-Karnataka (KKR) regional quota, plus a relaxed-eligibility (not seat-reservation) provision for Karnataka-domiciled SC/ST/OBC candidates |
| Approx. fee | ~₹1.25 lakh/year (government-funded-quota seats at some colleges can be as low as ~₹50k/year) | ~₹2.2–3.5 lakh/year (commonly rounded to "~3 lakh/year" or "3x KCET") |
| Rank formula | **50% KCET exam score + 50% 2nd PUC/12th PCM marks** | Based solely on the COMEDK exam itself |
| Relative difficulty | Considered easier, closely follows the PUC/12th syllabus | Considered tougher/more conceptual, "closer to JEE Main level" |

**Group's own analogy:** *"KCET = Local train — cheap ticket, Karnataka residents only. COMEDK = Express train — costly ticket, open to all of India."* Because Karnataka-resident students are eligible for BOTH, they can in practice compete for up to ~75–80% of a private college's total seats, a real structural advantage over out-of-state candidates who only have COMEDK. **Guidance repeated throughout: it's the same college, same degree, same classroom, same placement cell regardless of route (KCET/COMEDK/Management/Government-funded) — the only real differences are fee and competition level.**

### 7.2 Domicile — when it actually matters (the single most over-asked topic in the group)

**For KCET**, most students don't need a separately-issued domicile certificate at all — the system auto-verifies Karnataka study history via **SATS** (Student Achievement Tracking System, Karnataka's state student-records database):
- **Clause A** (the default/standard route): 7 years of Karnataka study (class 1–12) including at least one board exam in Karnataka — verified automatically via SATS wherever possible.
- **Clause B**: for students who don't personally meet the 7-year rule but qualify based on a PARENT's long-term Karnataka domicile/study history instead.
- Further lettered clauses (C through Y) cover other edge cases (returning NRIs, parental-employment-based eligibility, etc.) — always check the current year's KCET Information Brochure's clause table for the specific scenario, since this is detailed and family-specific.
- **Important:** these clauses only determine exam-sitting eligibility — they have **no bearing on later seat allocation**, which runs purely on KCET rank + whichever reservation category is separately claimed.

**For COMEDK**, domicile is irrelevant for the vast majority of applicants. It only matters in exactly two scenarios: (1) claiming **KKR (Kalyana-Karnataka) quota** — needs both an Article-371J KKR certificate AND a Karnataka Domicile Certificate; (2) claiming the **relaxed minimum-eligibility marks** for Karnataka-domiciled SC/ST/OBC candidates (40% PCM aggregate instead of the standard 45%). If a candidate is comfortably above 45% anyway, there's no reason to pursue a domicile certificate. Choosing "By Birth" vs "By Domicile" vs "Non-Karnataka" in the COMEDK form makes **no other practical difference.**

**How to get a Karnataka Domicile Certificate** (community-compiled steps): (1) get a Study Certificate from the school with the most years attended; (2) have Aadhaar (long-form, showing issue date) or a passport proving 7+ years' stay; (3) prepare a ₹100 stamp-paper affidavit (father typically provides this); (4) get all documents notarized; (5) submit at the Deputy Tehsildar office (~₹25 fee); (6) collect an acknowledgment; (7) collect the certificate after ~14 days — **valid for only 1 year.** Real experiences varied widely by local office — some parents were turned away or given a shorter-duration certificate than requested; Nadakacheri/Sevasindhu online portals sometimes failed to fetch Aadhaar-linked data.

### 7.3 SATS / study-certificate / BEO-DDPI verification — precise mechanics

- If a candidate accumulates **7 SATS-verified (green-tick) years, including one from 10th standard**, Clause-A eligibility is fully satisfied — no study certificate or signature needed at all. Roughly 90% of applicants reportedly fall into this easy path.
- If SATS auto-fetch fails for some years (very commonly 11th/12th, since schools often haven't updated records mid-term), those years show as "not verified" (red) — the candidate manually enters them and later gets them verified once KEA opens a verification window for that school.
- **BEO (Block Education Officer)** signs study certificates for **up to 10th standard only**; **DDPI/DDPU** (Deputy Director, Pre-University) signs for **PU/11th-12th**. Each school has its own zone-specific BEO/DDPI office — no universal answer; check with the specific issuing school.
- **CBSE/ICSE certificates officially do NOT need a BEO signature** — but in practice, some verification staff mistakenly insist anyway. Getting the signature if asked is typically a fast (~5-minute), low-friction process — not worth pre-emptively worrying about.
- A genuinely reassuring real data point: RVCE reportedly did not ask Round-1 joiners for study certificates or BEO signatures at all in 2026, reinforcing that KEA itself is the final verifying authority once a rank/seat is issued.
- Distinct documents, easily confused: the **Verification Slip** (downloadable once documents are confirmed verified — the key green light to proceed to choice-filling), the **Rank Card** (downloaded post-result; some colleges like PES want this in a specific format), and the **application printout** itself.

### 7.4 KCET exam format & day-of specifics (paper-based OMR — different from JEE/BITSAT's computer format)

- PCM compulsory; Biology (or another 4th subject) is optional and only relevant for non-Engineering course targets — Engineering-only aspirants can skip it, and an admit card with no Biology signature is completely normal.
- Question booklet ~15–20 pages, bilingual (Kannada + English); sealed at first — students fill OMR header fields for the first 10 minutes before being allowed to break the seal.
- **Only ball pens for OMR bubbling — no gel pens.** Recommended tip size 0.7mm–1mm; have the student test a few in advance for speed and clean bubble-filling.
- **Bring a long ruler/scale** to cleanly break the booklet seal without damaging pages — some centres disallow it inside (leave it outside the room in that case), but most centres have allowed it in past years.
- A **carbon copy of the OMR answer sheet** is given to take home for self-verification — it's often faint/hard to read, which is normal.
- **Use the SAME physical admit card across all papers** (PCM, +B if opted) — invigilators sign it session-by-session, and colleges will ask to see this signed original later during admission, so keep it very safe.
- Post-exam, retrieve from the hall: the signed hall ticket, the question booklet, the answer-sheet carbon copy, and your own pen/scale/Aadhaar.
- **Reporting time**: admit cards state "2 hours before the first bell," which many parents found excessive in practice (commonly targeting ~9:00–9:15 AM arrival instead) — but err on the side of the official instruction, especially for an unfamiliar centre, given real traffic-day horror stories (one parent's car got stuck 1.6km from the centre with minutes to spare; they ran the rest of the way and were saved by a stranger's lift).
- **Dress code** is often not clearly published on KEA's website despite the admit card referring there — check page 2 of the admit card itself instead (no jewellery, no full sleeves commonly cited; one specific centre strictly enforced no jeans/closed shoes, slippers/bare feet only). Safest bet: follow standard JEE-style norms (light clothing, minimal footwear, no jewellery) even where KCET's own instructions are vague.
- **Exam-centre allocation will not be changed for being "too far"** — KEA/COMEDK explicitly will not accommodate such requests at scale. Real examples: a Bangalore COMEDK candidate who listed 3 Bangalore-area city preferences was allotted a centre in Mangaluru; another was allotted Chennai. Always verify the EXACT centre address on the admit card (multiple centres can share a similar name/locality, e.g., three different PU colleges in Kengeri alone).

### 7.5 KCET rank mechanics — the 50/50 formula and multi-board timing complexity

- **Final KCET rank = 50% KCET exam marks + 50% 2nd PUC/12th PCM marks.** This is why the process has two stages: sit the KCET exam (April), then separately upload 12th board marks on a KEA "marks entry portal" once results are out.
- **KEA does NOT wait for every board** — Karnataka PU results normally arrive first (April), and KEA computes the main rank list off that batch, typically releasing results (with subject-wise marks AND rank together, not marks-then-rank) around the last week of May.
- **Non-PU boards (CBSE, ICSE, others) get a LATER, separate marks-upload window**, opened only after each board's own results (and any revaluation/improvement results) are out — informally running roughly July–September. Ranks recalculated this way get an **alphabet suffix** (e.g., "2100A") signaling a post-revision rank to colleges during counselling. A rank can move up OR down; Round 1 college cutoffs themselves don't retroactively change, but an individual's own later-round eligibility can.
- KCET has **no negative marking** (1 mark per correct answer), so once the final answer key is out, families can self-estimate scores fairly reliably ahead of the official result.
- There's no official minimum "good score" per subject (unlike JEE Advanced's CRL threshold) — it depends entirely on target college/branch and that year's paper difficulty, which varies significantly year to year.

### 7.6 KCET/COMEDK counselling mechanics — choices, rounds, and fee-payment rules (materially different between the two bodies — a very common source of confusion)

**KCET choice options after an allotment:**
1. **Accept & Freeze (Choice 1)** — pay fees, report, exit counselling.
2. **Accept & Upgrade (Choice 2 / "Hold and upgrade")** — seat guaranteed (this or better) while trying for an upgrade next round. **No fee payment required for Choice 2** — confirmed multiple times and directly per the official seat-allotment brochure; this often confused parents because the portal sometimes showed a misleading "payment pending" status.
3. **Reject & Upgrade (Choice 3)** — don't lock in the current seat, retain the full original choice list, try fresh next round.
4. **Reject & Withdraw (Choice 4)** — exit KCET entirely.
- A **Pre-Round** (ahead of main Round 1, mainly benefiting early-ranked CBSE/other-board candidates) offers only two options: accept-pay-exit, or reject and go into Round 1 completely fresh with **no hold-and-upgrade available at Pre-Round stage** — a genuinely debated fairness point in the group (see §11 below for the full worked-example discussion).
- Seats obtained in an **Extended Round (Round 3)** carry a stronger commitment expectation — KEA reportedly pursues legal action against candidates who get an extended-round seat and simply don't take it.

**COMEDK choice options — a stricter, different fee model than KCET:**
1. **Accept & Freeze** — pay full fee, report.
2. **Accept & Upgrade** — **unlike KCET, this REQUIRES full fee payment upfront in COMEDK.**
3. **Reject & Upgrade** — **also requires full fee payment upfront**, even though the seat is being rejected — a major, repeatedly-confirmed difference from KCET's no-payment Choice 3.
4. **Reject & Withdraw** — the only no-payment exit option.
- COMEDK's own Counselling Process Document states plainly: *"If you are allotted a seat and make any of [Accept & Freeze / Accept & Upgrade / Reject & Upgrade] you must pay the total fee of the allotted course/college."*
- **Payment must be done on desktop/laptop — COMEDK's official instructions explicitly say "Do NOT use Mobile phone."** Ensure sufficient bank balance AND a high enough single-transaction limit before the payment window (contact your bank in advance if needed) — no extension is granted for payment failures.
- **Seat cancellation in COMEDK is only allowed in Round 1, Round 2 (KKR seats only), and Round 3** — after each round's specific cancellation deadline, requests aren't entertained.
- **Round 2 in COMEDK is reserved for KKR candidates only** — General Merit candidates go straight from Round 1 to Round 3 in most recent cycles (exact round-numbering has shifted year to year — confirm current terminology each cycle).
- **Round 4 "Reject & Exit" availability is conditional**: candidates never previously allotted any seat (R1/R2/R3) retain a Reject & Exit option in Round 4; candidates who WERE previously allotted and are in Round 4 either upgrading or seeking a fresh allotment do **not** have that exit option if Round 4 gives them anything.
- **Real seat-allotment computation logic (confirmed after an in-group debate/correction)**: for each choice in a candidate's list, the system checks General Merit (GM) FIRST at that specific choice; only if GM fails does it check the candidate's reservation category (e.g., 3BH) at that SAME choice, then further horizontal sub-categories (rural/Kannada-medium/women, etc.); only if nothing succeeds does it move to the next choice and repeat. **It does NOT scan all 200 choices for GM before ever touching reservation categories** — the GM→category cascade happens choice-by-choice, top to bottom. Getting a GM seat despite holding a reservation category is common and generally GOOD news (better college/branch, reservation benefit stays banked for later rounds).

### 7.7 Fee mechanics, the KEA "escrow" model, and cross-counselling refund risk

- For KCET seats, the ~₹1–1.2 lakh tuition portion is paid **to KEA** (not the college directly) — KEA acts like an escrow, transferring funds to the college only after counselling concludes. A smaller ~₹20,000 "lab/skill fee" is paid **directly to the college** at reporting. When upgrading rounds, KEA automatically handles the larger tuition-portion transfer/adjustment; the smaller college-level fee is NOT automatically handled — check with the specific college.
- **KCET and COMEDK are entirely separate, unconnected bodies — there is NO automatic fee adjustment between a COMEDK-paid seat and a later KCET-allotted seat**, even at the same college. A candidate must formally exit COMEDK and separately pay for KCET; getting the COMEDK fee refunded requires actively initiating a refund request (send the KCET allotment letter + COMEDK fee receipt to COMEDK's refund email).
- **Cross-body deadline risk is real and financially significant**: AICTE's own deadline (2026: 20 July) can cause candidates to forfeit fees already paid at AICTE-affiliated private universities (e.g., Manipal) if they wait for a later KCET seat. VIT's stated policy: no refund after 30 days from admission date — and KCET/COMEDK results (often landing in August) can arrive AFTER that window closes, risking a 50%+ fee loss.
- A more reassuring real pattern from a parent's own 2-years-prior experience: applying to 3 universities and withdrawing from 2 well before each one's own deadline resulted in a FULL tuition refund at both (though a separate "counselling fee" was non-refundable); refund speed varied significantly by institution — one processed almost instantly, another waited until all its own rounds concluded. **Hostel/deposit amounts are frequently NOT refunded even when tuition is** (one real example: 90% tuition refund but no hostel-deposit refund).
- Government-funded courses at some colleges (BMS, SJCE) run at roughly **half the fee** of the "normal" KCET-quota rate at the SAME college — but these government-funded seats are typically limited to specific, often older, non-CS branches; CSE and closely allied branches are usually NOT in the funded tier.

### 7.8 Seat matrix — what it is, and why its release is often delayed

- The "seat matrix" (government-approved seat counts per college/branch/category) must be finalized before choice-filling can meaningfully open. A "draft" version usually precedes the "final" one — don't finalize choices purely off the draft.
- **The 2026 cycle saw a real, significant seat-matrix-driven delay** to the entire counselling schedule, directly reported in local news.
- Colleges with both "aided" and "unaided" designations appear as **two separate entries/codes** in the seat matrix (e.g., BMSCE codes E003-aided vs E048-unaided) — a very common source of parents missing one entry while searching.
- A genuine unresolved discrepancy was flagged between KEA's official 2026 seat matrix numbers for PES and PES's own website intake figures — cross-check multiple sources and treat any single source as provisional until confirmed at admission.
- **A notable 2026 market-supply observation**: ~41,000 of ~81,000 total KEA-administered seats (roughly half) were CSE-or-allied — a real, openly-discussed concern among parents about long-term CS graduate oversupply, presented as an open question rather than a resolved fact.

### 7.9 Bangalore/Karnataka college & branch landscape — real comparisons from the group

- **Institutional autonomy types** matter for how current the curriculum stays: **Deemed University** (own curriculum/tests/degree, e.g., VIT) > **Autonomous college** (own curriculum/tests, degree from a parent university, e.g., Dayananda Sagar College of Engineering) > **Affiliated college** (follows the university's set curriculum, e.g., RVITM, BMSIT, Sir MVIT — all VTU-affiliated).
- **UVCE (University Visvesvaraya College of Engineering)** — a detailed, strongly positive profile shared by an admin worth highlighting since it corrects a common "government college = lesser" assumption: founded 1917 by Sir M. Visvesvaraya, older than the IITs, a full Bangalore University constituent college. **KCET 2024 CSE cutoff ≈ rank 400–800** — closing right alongside RVCE, ahead of PES/BMS/MSRIT. Fees are dramatically lower (~₹49,600/year, ~₹1.98L for all 4 years) than any comparable private option (RV/BMS/MSRIT COMEDK ~₹10–11L total; PES ~₹19.2L; Amrita ~₹24.1L; SRM ~₹22.1L). 2024 placement data: 84% placed, median ₹8.5 LPA (above SRM/MSRIT, close to BMSCE), highest ₹26.5 LPA that year. Genuine downsides: aging infrastructure, no AC labs, zero marketing/branding, limited hostel capacity (many day-scholars), some faculty vacancies. Genuine strengths: a 100+ year alumni network (CEOs, ISRO scientists, IAS officers), a prime central-Bangalore location, an elite peer group, lower attendance pressure enabling more independent coding/internship pursuit. **No Management Quota route exists at UVCE** (fully government). Admin's bottom line: "the IIT of Karnataka state colleges — brilliant but underfunded"; a strong pick for KCET rank <1,000, cost-sensitive, self-motivated students.
- **PES University runs up to 4 separate/parallel admission tracks** into overlapping seats: JEE-Main-based, PESU-KCET (PES's own separate KCET-linked counselling, calculating its OWN internal rank off the KCET score + a 60% 12th-PCM minimum, deliberately starting only AFTER KEA's Round 1), regular KEA/KCET counselling, and Management Quota — a persistent, major source of parent confusion throughout the 2026 cycle.
  - **PESU-KCET reserved only ~5% of total PES seats in 2026** and its fee (~₹5.5 lakh/year for CSE) is dramatically higher than the standard KEA-KCET route (~₹1.5 lakh/year for a comparable college) — a major decision factor beyond just college/branch.
  - **PESU-KCET → Government-KCET conversion policy**: PES will refund the PESU-KCET amount if a candidate later secures a Government-KCET seat at PES for the EXACT SAME campus and specialization — but this commitment does **not** extend to a later COMEDK-based seat, and how strictly "same campus/specialization" is enforced was flagged as genuinely unclear — make a fully-informed choice before committing to PESU-KCET.
  - **JEE-based PES seat → Government-KCET conversion (a separate, distinct scenario)**: PES has committed that a JEE-based seat converts to a KCET-based one (with a fee-difference refund) if the KCET allotment is at the SAME PES campus and branch — confirmed independently by multiple parents. PES's hard deadline for submitting original documents or withdrawing a JEE-based seat was **15 July 2026** — notably BEFORE KCET's own Round 1 results that cycle, creating real anxiety; PES's guidance was to keep the JEE-based seat as an active backup and let conversion happen automatically once KCET comes through.
  - **Important naming trap**: "PES College of Engineering, Mandya" is a completely different institution from "PES University, Bangalore" (EC or RR campus) — several parents mistakenly selected the Mandya college while choice-filling.
  - **PESU RR Campus EEE vs NIE Mysore EEE** (a detailed real comparison an admin built for one family's decision): PESU RR — ₹16.6L/4yrs, avg package ₹8.5–10 LPA, Tier-1 private Bangalore brand, high software-role flexibility (CSE minor allowed), 100+/year go abroad for MS. NIE Mysore — ₹3.2L/4yrs (govt quota), avg package ₹4.5–6 LPA, strong pure-core legacy (75+ years), better GATE-coaching/PSU-alumni culture, lower cost of living. Choose PESU RR if budget isn't a constraint and software+core flexibility or a US-MS plan matters; choose NIE if fees matter most or the goal is a strictly core-EEE/PSU/GATE path.
- **Branch-naming confusion, genuinely important**: **ECE and ETE (Electronics & Telecommunication) are NOT the same branch** — ECE has consistently high demand and broad scope; ETE is narrower and has seen declining enrollment/discontinuation at several colleges. One student-sourced account claimed ETE might close within 2-3 years at a specific college with weak current placements; this was directly disputed by another admin who argued the underlying field (mobile telephony, 5G/6G infrastructure) remains genuinely strong and that declining popularity reflects branding/perception (people gravitate to the broader "ECE" label) more than actual field prospects. **Presented as a genuinely unresolved disagreement between two informed sources, not settled fact.**
- Real 2025 ECE cutoff data points (KCET, General Merit): RVCE ≈ 1,100–1,200 | BMS ≈ 3,100 | PES Ring Road ≈ 3,700 | BMSCE ≈ 4,700 (some sources). UVCE core CS closed at 3,473 (Round 1) → 5,020 (Round 3) in 2025. **For precise, branch-by-branch 2026 Round 1 cutoffs across the Top 50 colleges, see the detailed dataset in §7.12.**
- **A recurring, useful methodological caution**: a college with dramatically more seats in a branch will mechanically show a "worse" (higher/later) closing rank even at similar or better relative demand — always check seat COUNT alongside the cutoff rank (e.g., BMS's very high CSE seat count, after merging several CS-allied branches into core CS, naturally pushed its closing rank later without necessarily reflecting weaker demand).
- **Always trust COMEDK's own official cutoff PDFs over a college's self-published notice board data** — real discrepancies have been found where colleges mistakenly blended KKR-quota admits into their displayed "GM" figures.

### 7.10 Choice-filling strategy, official cutoff sources, and round-reliability guidance

- **Ground rules from an admin**: put genuine dream choices at the TOP of the list regardless of last year's cutoff (costs nothing — the algorithm tries top-down and stops at the first match); include realistic/safety choices further down to guarantee an acceptable worst case; unless fully satisfied with an early-round outcome, it's generally worth continuing through to Round 3 since cutoffs keep shifting favorably as others exit/upgrade.
- **"Round 2 is safe"** was the group's practical answer on which round's cutoffs to trust when choice-filling (Round 3+ cutoffs can move further).
- **A costly, easy-to-make ordering mistake**: if a candidate prefers College-A's branch over College-B's same branch but accidentally lists College-B ABOVE College-A, they may be allotted College-B even though their rank would have qualified for College-A — the system doesn't "look ahead," it locks the first match found in list order.
- **Official cutoff data sources**: KEA — `cetonline.karnataka.gov.in/kea/ugcet[year]` → "Seat Allotment / Cutoff" (college-wise/branch-wise/category-wise closing-rank PDFs per round). COMEDK — `comedk.org/counselling-document-2025` (or current year) → "Engineering Cut-off Ranks" per round.
- **Community-built tools** (unofficial, cross-check against official data): `entrance-exams.netlify.app` (an admin's cutoff analyzer covering KCET/COMEDK/JoSAA — click any college for all-branch cutoffs, or enter a rank to see what was available the prior year; explicitly flagged by its creator as unverified for every data point). **§7.12 below is built from this same underlying tracker/methodology, packaged as a static branch-by-branch Round 1 reference for the Top 50 colleges.**
- The group also ran an informal ~113-respondent survey comparing perceived difficulty of JEE Main vs. BITSAT vs. KCET across Physics/Maths/Chemistry, shared as an internal analysis PDF.

### 7.11 KCET Pre-Round fairness debate (worth preserving as a genuinely open question)

A parent raised a substantive concern using a real worked example (RVCE CSE/AIML seat math): since Pre-Round offers no hold-and-upgrade option, anxious candidates may over-accept Pre-Round seats they don't actually want most (fearing losing them), potentially exhausting seats before Round 1 opens for patiently-waiting candidates. An admin's mathematically-reasoned rebuttal: **as long as choice lists remain unchanged between Pre-Round and Round 1**, no one with a better rank can newly claim a seat you passed on, since they'd have had the same shot at it in Pre-Round already — the risk only becomes real if inter-round choice-list edits are allowed. Whether such edits are allowed was not definitively resolved in the transcript — a nuance worth clarifying directly with KEA each cycle rather than assuming either way.

### How to read this document

- **Rank** is the cutoff merit rank a candidate needed to be allotted that seat — **lower rank number = more competitive** (harder to get). A rank of 200 is far more competitive than a rank of 20,000.
- **"Tightened"** means the cutoff rank got lower (more competitive) between rounds; **"loosened"** means it got higher (more room, easier to get in). Only KCET has a single round so far, so movement is currently only computable for ComedK (Mock → Round 1).
- **Composite rank position** (used to pick this Top 50 list) is *not* simply "the college with the single best cutoff." It blends the **median cutoff of every branch category a college offers**, weighted by how often each category is a student's top choice system-wide (from actual 1st-choice cutoff patterns). A college with strong, broad-based demand across popular categories (e.g. CS) ranks ahead of one whose only standout is a single low-demand niche branch. This is the same method the live Dashboard calls "Composite score."
- **Merged rank** in this document combines KCET and ComedK standing: a college qualifies for the Top 50 if it ranks well in *either* exam (its merged position = the better of its two per-exam positions). KCET and ComedK evaluate different, differently-sized applicant pools, so their raw composite scores are not directly comparable — only each list's own ordinal position is used to merge.
- **College name matching** between the two exams (which use unrelated code systems) is done by normalized-name matching, verified to be conservative — a college with no confirmed match on the other exam shows "not matched" rather than risking a wrong pairing.
- **Branch categories:** Computer Science & IT, Electronics & Communication, Electrical & Electronics, Mechanical & Allied, Civil & Construction, Chemical & Polymer, Biotechnology & Life Sciences, Aerospace, Other Branches.
- **Duplicate-looking names:** KEA sometimes lists one physical college under more than one KCET code, each with its own cutoffs (likely different seat-matrix groupings — this data does not say which). Where that happens within this Top 50, both are kept as separate entries and disambiguated with their code, e.g. "B M S College of Engineering (KCET E003)" vs "(KCET E048)".

## 9. Counselling: JoSAA, CSAB, JAC Delhi, BITS Iterations

### 9.1 JoSAA — the core process (IITs + NIT+ System combined)
- **Registration** typically opens the day after the JEE Advanced result is declared. **2 rounds of Mock Allotment** happen before the real Round 1 (~10 days after registration opens). Architecture Aptitude Test (AAT)-qualified candidates join the process in this window too.
- **5 real rounds** in the 2026 cycle (down from a "usual 6" in some prior years — reason not stated in-thread). **Round 1 is the longest (~2 weeks)** due to heaviest document-verification load; subsequent rounds run **~4–6 days each.**
- **IITs finish allocating at the end of Round 5**; IIT reporting begins ~20 July onward (institute-specific dates). **NIT+ System (NITs/IIITs/GFTIs) candidates go through 2–3 more CSAB rounds in August** to fill vacancies.
- **Choice List mechanics:**
  - Choices should reflect true preference order — a "dream" option can sit at #1 even if unrealistic, since the algorithm scans top-to-bottom and stops at the **first available match**; a stretch choice at the top costs nothing.
  - Choice List **locks permanently** at a fixed cutoff time (2026: **11 June, 5 PM**) — no edits possible for the rest of the cycle. Mock Allotment 1/2 reflect only "as things currently stand" and are **not guaranteed** to match Round 1 reality, since other candidates' choices/entries can still change before lock.
  - **Real, irreversible mistakes cited as cautionary examples**: a parent meant to enter "IIT-M: Instrumentation and Biomedical Engineering" but "IIT-M: Biological Engineering" got entered/prioritized instead and was allotted; similarly, an NIT-Delhi course got entered instead of the intended IIT-Delhi course. **Both parent and child should independently review the full choice list before lock.**
  - Choice-list edits stop entirely once the actual round process starts; mock-round cutoff data is historically **not** released by JoSAA (only real-round OR-CR is).
- **State quota** (NITs/some GFTIs only — NOT IITs, NOT IIITs, which are 100% All-India merit): 50% Home State (HS) + 50% Other State (OS), based on the "State Code of Eligibility" = the state where the candidate's Class XII board exam was taken (NOT residence/domicile). No separate domicile certificate is needed — this is auto-derived from the 12th marksheet. A candidate is HS-quota-eligible in exactly ONE NIT (their own state's, if it has one) and OS-quota everywhere else. **IIIT-D is the one IIIT exception**, with its 85% Delhi / 15% Outside-Delhi split via JAC Delhi.
- **Freeze / Float / Slide options** (chosen after each allotment where you already hold a seat):
  - **Not choosing any of the three = automatic exit from counselling** (there's no separate "reject and exit" button — inaction is the exit mechanism).
  - Choice can generally be changed within a round, up to the end of that round's Online Reporting window.
  - Once "Slide" is chosen, the **next** round only allows Slide or Freeze — **cannot revert to Float.**
- **Fees (explicit numbers):**
  - **SAF (Seat Acceptance Fee)**, paid ONCE by a first-time allottee: **₹15,000 or ₹30,000** by category, which **includes a ₹5,000 JoSAA processing charge.** A later Float/Slide seat change does NOT trigger a repeat SAF payment (but a fresh "Willingness" confirmation is still required each round).
  - **PAF (Partial Admission Fee)**, payable by NIT+ System candidates at the end of Round 5 to retain their seat: **₹20,000 or ₹45,000** by category. 2026 window: 22–24 July, later extended to 25 July, 11 AM.
  - **Refund/deduction rules**: once SAF is paid, there is **never a full refund** thereafter. Seat cancellation for "process reasons" deducts ₹5,000; withdrawal or PAF non-payment deducts ₹7,000 (rest refunded).
  - **IIT withdrawal is only possible up to Round 4.** From Round 5 onward, a first-time IIT allotment is **deemed accepted even without physical/online reporting**, and the candidate becomes **ineligible for JEE Advanced the following year.**
  - **NIT+ System withdrawal remains possible even in the last round** (unlike IITs) — either explicit withdrawal in Round 5, or simply not paying the PAF (automatic cancellation, treated as rejection, **with no future-eligibility penalty** — such candidates fall into "Type-9" eligibility for CSAB Special Rounds and can freely reapply to JoSAA/CSAB the following year).
- **Documents typically needed**: Class XII marksheet (also the State-of-Eligibility proof), JEE Main admit card, category/caste certificate (if applicable), Medical Fitness Certificate (**only required post-allotment**, not at registration/choice-filling), bank account details, category certificates dated on/after 1 April of the admission year.
- **Category vs. Open-category interaction (a commonly misunderstood mechanic)**: a reserved-category candidate can still be allotted an **Open/General category seat** if their rank is strong enough — being in a reserved category never blocks access to Open seats. If allotted via Open, the corresponding reserved-quota seat is **not** consumed and remains available for the next reserved-category candidate. Only if Open-category allotment isn't possible does the system check the candidate's own reserved-category quota.
- Roughly **30% of total NIT+ System seats** were reportedly vacant at the end of JoSAA in a recent year (not evenly distributed — varies heavily by branch/institute) — these vacancies feed into CSAB.

### 9.2 CSAB (fills NIT+ System vacancies after JoSAA)
- Separate portal (csab.nic.in); registration opens a few days after all JoSAA rounds conclude (~end of July/early August).
- **JoSAA's choice list does NOT carry over** — CSAB requires a fresh choice-filling, regardless of whether a candidate got a JoSAA seat or not.
- Candidates who retained a JoSAA NIT+ seat by paying PAF and then also register for CSAB get their **current seat auto-entered as the LAST choice**, with room to add upgrade options above it. All prior SAF/PAF payments carry forward (minus processing fees); an additional **"IAF-2"** payment is required during CSAB.
- Registration for CSAB is a distinct, explicit step — it is not automatic just because a candidate has (or lacks) a JoSAA seat.

### 9.3 JAC Delhi (covers IIIT-D, DTU, NSUT)
- Registration typically opens ~last week of May. Runs its own schedule and Business Rules document, separate from JoSAA.
- Unofficial JAC Delhi cutoff predictor tool referenced informally: jac-cutoff-and-predictor.netlify.app.

### 9.4 BITS Iterations
See §5.5 above for full detail — key summary: runs on its own separate portal/schedule (not JoSAA-linked), called "Iterations" rather than "Rounds," and overlaps heavily in timing with JoSAA, forcing many families to make BITS accept/float decisions before knowing their JoSAA outcome.

## 12. Exam-Day Logistics (Bangalore/Karnataka-region practical knowledge)

1. **City/centre allotment is not fully in a family's control and CAN change late** — most dramatically for BITSAT, but also seen with JEE Main (a city-intimation slip showing one city, admit card showing another, forcing last-minute travel replanning). **Never finalize non-refundable travel/hotel bookings until the admit card itself is downloaded, and re-check it again close to the exam date.**
2. **Parking is the single most common complaint** at Bangalore-area centres, especially industrial-area/IT-park venues (Peenya, Harohalli, Rain Cloud Technologies, etc.) — most have little to no formal parking; families typically use the main road or an adjacent open ground (sometimes later closed off). Arrive early specifically to secure parking.
3. **Metro + last-mile auto/cab is a reliable fallback** where parking is scarce (Yelachenahalli, Jalahalli, Kengeri, Challaghatta stops were mentioned as workable for specific centres).
4. **Arrive well before the printed reporting time.** Gates commonly close ~30 minutes before the exam start (driven by biometric-verification cutoffs) — the admit card's "reporting time" is generally NOT the "last entry" time. General advice: 45–90 minutes early depending on distance/parking uncertainty.
5. **No re-entry after biometric verification** — a mid-exam bio-break means re-frisking and re-biometric checks (real time lost), so plan water intake sensibly beforehand.
6. **Parent waiting facilities vary hugely** — some centres offer shaded seating, water, restrooms, nearby cafés; others have literally no space, causing crowding right at the gate. Common tip: wait ~5–10 minutes after drop-off (in case the child needs to hand back a forgotten item like jewellery/extra photo), then move away from the immediate gate area to ease congestion, returning closer to pickup time.
7. **Large campuses can have multiple buildings/gates** — always confirm the exact building from the admit card (some "iON Digital Zone"-branded venues share a campus with an unrelated college, causing confusion about which building actually hosts the test).
8. **Out-of-city allotments happen and can be far** — Bangalore-based BITSAT candidates were sometimes sent to Shimoga, Hubballi, or Mysuru. Crowd-sourced hotel tips from the group: **Hubballi** — The Bliss, Hotel President, Naveen, Kailash Prabhat, Hotel Ashoka, Denison (near Akshay Colony); nowhere in Hubballi is more than ~30 min from anywhere else. **Mysuru** — Ginger (~25 min from one centre); check proximity to your specific centre. **Shimoga** — no strong hotel consensus in-thread; similarly compact city (<30 min anywhere).
9. **Dress-code and prohibited-items list applies broadly across JEE Main, JEE Advanced, and BITSAT**: no watches (an on-screen timer is provided), no jewellery, no caps/scarves/hoodies/jackets, no belts with large buckles/metallic clothing, no phones/calculators/earphones/Bluetooth/bags/wallets (most centres offer no storage), no food. Light clothing, simple slip-on footwear speeds up frisking.
10. **Carry the ORIGINAL Aadhaar**, not a photocopy (at least one student was turned away and sent to fetch the original); pre-unlock Aadhaar biometrics via the mAadhaar app before exam day.
11. **Memory-based "reconstructed" question papers circulate within hours** of any shift, compiled by coaching institutes from students' recollections — useful for rough difficulty-gauging, but NOT an official released paper. (BITS never releases papers/keys at all, unlike NTA which does officially publish JEE Main answer keys.)
12. **Answer-key/score discrepancies happen every cycle** — dropped/erroneous questions in specific shifts, and (for BITSAT) unexplained score reductions, are recurring frustrations; the group's practical tactic was to email with a clear, consistent subject line so a high volume of similarly-worded complaints is more likely to prompt a response.

**KCET/COMEDK-specific additions (from the dedicated KCET/COMEDK source group):**

13. **KCET is paper-based OMR, not computer-based** — this changes the exam-day kit materially. Only ball pens for bubbling (no gel pens); recommended tip size 0.7mm–1mm, tested in advance. Bring a long ruler/scale to cleanly break the sealed question booklet's seal (most centres allowed it in past years; if disallowed, leave it just outside the room). A carbon copy of the OMR answer sheet is given to take home (often faint — that's normal). Use the SAME physical admit card across every KCET paper, since invigilators sign it session-by-session, and colleges ask to see the signed original later at admission — keep it very safe.
14. **A biometric/face-scan step exists at KCET centres too**, not just JEE/BITSAT — keep a soft (digital) copy of the admit card on your phone as backup in case the scan struggles and a photo needs to be produced instead.
15. **KCET/COMEDK centre allocation is just as "not up for negotiation" as BITSAT's** — real 2026 examples included a Bangalore-preference COMEDK candidate sent to Mangaluru, and another sent to Chennai. Always verify the exact centre address on the admit card, since multiple centres can share a similar name/locality (three separate PU colleges in Kengeri alone, for instance).
16. **KCET's admit card "reporting time" (2 hours before the first bell) is widely felt to be excessive** in practice (many aim for ~9:00–9:15 AM instead) — but a real cautionary story is worth heeding: one parent's car got stuck 1.6km from the centre in traffic with minutes to spare; they abandoned the car and ran, saved only by a stranger's spontaneous lift. Build in generous travel buffer, especially for unfamiliar routes.
17. **KCET dress-code instructions are often not on KEA's website despite the admit card referring there** — check page 2 of the admit card itself instead. One centre strictly enforced no jeans/closed shoes (slippers or bare feet only that day) — given inconsistent official guidance, following standard JEE-style norms (light clothing, no jewellery, minimal footwear) is the safest default.
18. **Parent waiting-area quality varies just as much at KCET/COMEDK centres as at JEE/BITSAT ones** — some provide indoor seating with snacks available, others leave parents on an open footpath with no shade.
19. **Document-verification slot booking is a separate step from admit-card download**, relevant only to candidates whose SATS auto-verification didn't fully go through — an online slot-booking link (on the KEA UGCET portal) lets a candidate pick an offline verification date/shift at a KEA office; the actual in-person appointment is generally reported as quick and smooth once booked, even though finding the right link/process beforehand causes more parent anxiety than the appointment itself.

## 13. Common Parent FAQs (culled from repeated questions across the transcript)

**Q: Is there a minimum percentage required in each of Physics, Chemistry, and Maths individually to get into an NIT/IIIT?**
A: No. JoSAA's rule is ≥75% *aggregate* in Class XII (SC/ST/PwD: ≥65%) OR being in the top 20 percentile of your board — there is no individual subject-wise cutoff. (BITS is different: 75% aggregate PCM/B **with at least 60% in each subject** individually.)

**Q: My child qualified for JEE Advanced — does that guarantee an IIT seat?**
A: No. Qualifying (crossing the category cutoff) only makes a candidate eligible to compete; a meaningful IIT/branch outcome in the General category generally needs a rank within roughly the top ~20,000 (tighter for a specific IIT/branch preference), per admin guidance.

**Q: Does IIIT-Bangalore use JEE Advanced rank preferentially over JEE Main rank?**
A: No — it uses "Best JEE Rank" (BJR), simply the numerically better of a candidate's Main and Advanced ranks (if they have both). Neither exam is weighted higher; it's a pure numeric comparison.

**Q: Is there a state/regional quota at IIITs, like there is at NITs?**
A: No (with the sole exception of IIIT-Delhi, which reserves 85% of seats for Delhi candidates via JAC Delhi). All other IIITs, and all IITs, allocate 100% by All-India merit.

**Q: We're from Karnataka — do we need a domicile certificate to claim NIT Home State quota?**
A: No separate certificate is needed. JoSAA auto-derives "Home State" from the state where the candidate's Class XII board exam was taken (the "State Code of Eligibility"), verifiable via the 12th marksheet alone.

**Q: If my child is in a reserved category, can they still get a general/open seat?**
A: Yes — being in a reserved category never blocks access to Open-category seats if the rank is strong enough. If allotted via Open, the reserved-quota seat isn't consumed and stays available for the next eligible candidate.

**Q: What happens if we don't pay the JoSAA Partial Admission Fee (PAF) after Round 5?**
A: For NIT+ System seats, non-payment is treated as a rejection — the seat is cancelled automatically, with **no future-eligibility penalty** (the candidate can still apply to JoSAA/CSAB again next year, and remains eligible for CSAB Special Rounds this year, under "Type-9" eligibility). This is different from IITs, where a Round-5 allotment is deemed accepted with no exit option, and makes the candidate ineligible for JEE Advanced next year.

**Q: Can we still change our JoSAA choice list after Round 1 starts?**
A: No. The choice list locks completely at the pre-announced cutoff time (11 June 2026, 5 PM in this cycle) and is used unchanged for all subsequent rounds.

**Q: Is BITSAT scored/ranked like JEE Main (percentile per shift)?**
A: No. BITS does not publish rank or percentile — cutoffs are quoted in raw marks. Each candidate gets a uniquely-generated question paper, and BITS does not normalize across shifts the way NTA does for JEE (aside from occasional post-hoc corrections for verified erroneous questions).

**Q: Our child skipped BITSAT Session 1 — any downside?**
A: No specific documented penalty; the only trade-off is having just one data point (Session 2 only) rather than a best-of-two comparison. Some families deliberately skipped a session to save the fee, based on their own cutoff-fit analysis.

**Q: How is the BITS Dual-Degree branch (BE) decided?**
A: At BITSAT-admission time, dual-degree candidates are placed into an MSc branch only. The BE branch is decided after 1st year based on CGPA + preference, competing only against other dual-degree (MSc) students — not against direct-BE admits.

**Q: Do we need a Karnataka domicile certificate for KCET?**
A: Usually no. Most candidates' 7-year Karnataka study history is auto-verified via SATS (Clause A) with no separate certificate needed. A certificate is only relevant for edge cases like Clause B (parent-based eligibility) or certain reserved-category claims — check the current year's Info Bulletin clause table for your specific situation.

**Q: Do we need a Karnataka domicile certificate for COMEDK?**
A: For the vast majority of applicants, no — COMEDK is all-India merit with no domicile requirement. It only matters if claiming (a) KKR/Kalyana-Karnataka quota, or (b) the relaxed 40%-vs-45% PCM eligibility marks available to Karnataka-domiciled SC/ST/OBC candidates. If your child comfortably clears 45% PCM, don't bother pursuing a certificate for COMEDK.

**Q: In KCET, does choosing "Accept & Upgrade" (Choice 2) require paying fees immediately?**
A: No — this is KCET-specific and confirmed multiple times: Choice 2 guarantees the seat (or better) with **no fee payment required** in that round. This is a key difference from COMEDK, where the equivalent "Accept & Upgrade" AND even "Reject & Upgrade" both require full fee payment upfront.

**Q: My child's KCET rank looks worse than expected because they're shown in General Merit (GM) despite holding a reservation category — did something go wrong?**
A: No — this is expected system behavior, not an error. The system checks GM first at each choice in your list; only if GM isn't available does it fall through to your reservation category at that same choice. Getting a GM seat usually means a BETTER outcome (a more-preferred college/branch than the reservation route would have given), and the reservation benefit stays available for future rounds.

## 14. Emotional & Wellbeing Guidance (worth preserving as tone/approach guidance for next year's parents)

Multiple times across the 11-month cycle — after tough JEE Main shifts, after a brutal BITSAT session, after a JEE Advanced miss — parents in visible distress found a consistent, admin-endorsed support pattern. Consolidated:

1. **Let the child feel it.** Give space for the full range of emotions (anger, tears, frustration) for the rest of that day without rushing them past it.
2. **Redirect gently, the next day.** Shift focus toward whatever exams/opportunities remain rather than dwelling on the setback.
3. **Normalize the outcome.** Remind them explicitly that a huge number of equally hard-working peers experienced the same shift-difficulty "lottery" — exam-day variance (which shift, which random peer cohort, minor mistakes, even exam-centre factors) genuinely affects outcomes somewhat independent of true ability or effort. It is not a verdict on their capability.
4. **Keep Plan B/C visibly alive.** A repeat attempt next year, a different but still strong college, or the Masters route later are all real, valid paths — and kids "year after year happily settle" wherever they land, whether it was Plan A, B, or C.
5. **Parents should regulate their own visible anxiety.** Kids pick up on parental stress; admins repeatedly advised parents to "stand strong" and support without becoming emotional themselves, while acknowledging this is genuinely hard for parents too.
6. One admin's direct words to preserve verbatim as a model response: *"One exam does not define your ability. It's easy to handle success. How you handle disappointments when you fall a little short of your goal despite all the work is what defines you... Ultimate aim is not about a great course, great college, placement or lot of money. It's about being happy every moment of every day."*

This is not clinical advice — it is peer-to-peer parent support language that repeatedly resonated in the group and is worth echoing in tone when addressing a distressed parent or student in this context.

## 16. Limitations of This Knowledge Base

1. **All specific dates, fees, and cutoffs above are from the 2025–26 admission cycle.** Every one of these changes annually — this document tells you the *pattern and sequence*, not next year's exact numbers. Always direct parents to the official site for current-cycle specifics.
2. **KCET and COMEDK now have deep, detailed coverage** (§7, including a precise Top-50-college branch-level cutoff dataset at §7.12), drawn from a second, dedicated Margdarshak KCET/COMEDK transcript plus a standalone cutoff-data source. **VITEEE, SRMJEEE, MET/Manipal, and AEEE/Amrita remain only lightly covered** (§8), since even the dedicated KCET/COMEDK group deliberately routed those discussions to a separate, further sister group. Do not treat that continued absence as evidence they're less important — it reflects source availability, not the exams' significance.
3. **Qualitative college/branch comparisons in §7.9 and §11 are parent/admin opinions**, not official rankings or verified data — always flag them as such when relaying to a parent. Where two sources genuinely disagreed (e.g., the ETE-branch-prospects debate in §7.9), both sides are preserved rather than resolved.
4. **The §7.12 cutoff dataset is AI-assisted extraction from official KEA/COMEDK publications, not independently re-verified row by row**, per its own source disclaimer — treat every individual rank as needing a cross-check against the official publication before use in a real decision. It also covers **General Merit (GM) category only** and **Round 1 (plus ComedK Mock) only** — reserved-category cutoffs and later-round movement aren't in this dataset; later rounds typically loosen most cutoffs further as higher-ranked candidates upgrade out (see §7.10).
5. **BITS-related and KCET/COMEDK-related operational complaints (city allocation, score discrepancies, seat-matrix delays, admit-card errors) reflect one specific admission cycle (2026)** and may not recur in future years — but the *category* of risk (state/private-body counselling can be less standardized/transparent than NTA's government process) is a durable, reusable insight.
6. This document does not cover NEET/medical admissions, architecture-specific (NATA/JEE Paper 2) processes in depth, or international-university options beyond passing mentions.
