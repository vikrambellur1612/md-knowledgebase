# NEET UG & Medical College Admissions in India — A Margdarshak Reference Knowledge Base (2026 Edition)

## TL;DR

- **NEET UG 2026 was thrown into chaos:** the original 3 May 2026 exam was cancelled over a paper leak, a “Re-NEET” was held on 21 June 2026, results came on 16 July 2026 (11.21 lakh of ~20 lakh qualified), and the entire counselling calendar slid to an August–October window (MCC AIQ Round 1 from 4 August 2026).
- **The admission architecture is unchanged:** 15% All India Quota via MCC (mcc.nic.in) plus 85% state quota via each state’s authority (KEA in Karnataka), with deemed/central institutions and AYUSH counselled through their own bodies — so parents must track two-to-three portals simultaneously.
- **India now has 1,29,026 MBBS seats across 808 colleges** (government ≈ 63,700 / ~49%, private + deemed ≈ 65,300 / ~51%) against 22,79,743 registered candidates — meaning the real contest is for the ~63,000 government seats, and rank-to-seat mapping is brutally steep beyond the top ~50,000 ranks.

## Key Findings

**1. The 2026 disruption was a paper leak, not a technical glitch.** The National Testing Agency (NTA) cancelled the 3 May 2026 exam after Rajasthan Police’s Special Operations Group (SOG) found a “guess paper” with 140 questions matching the actual NEET paper. The CBI took over the probe, arrested five people across Jaipur, Nashik and Gurugram, and traced the leak to a multi-state “solver/leak” network that circulated the paper via WhatsApp/Telegram, coaching networks and couriers across Rajasthan, Haryana and Maharashtra. The Supreme Court declined to halt the re-exam or force a computer-based format.

**2. Re-NEET 2026 was conducted 21 June 2026 (2:00–5:15 PM, pen-and-paper, 13 languages)** with the same syllabus/pattern and no re-registration or extra fee. Results (16 July 2026): joint AIR-1 Aryan Gupta (Punjab) and Panshul Bansal (Haryana), each 715/720; 11.21 lakh qualified; >58% of qualifiers women; 19 candidates scored 700+, and 138 scored ≥690.

**3. The qualifying cutoff rose sharply because the re-exam was rated easier** — the General/EWS 50th-percentile band moved to 715–213 (from 686–144 in 2025), a jump of ~69 marks at the lower bound.

**4. Counselling for 2026 is compressed into Aug–Oct.** MCC AIQ Round 1: 4–17 August; Round 2: 24 Aug–2 Sept; Round 3: 10–18 Sept; Stray Vacancy: 28 Sept–3 Oct.  Karnataka/KEA ran a fresh registration window 17–24 July 2026 and began Round 1 option entry in August.

**5. NEET became mandatory for Allied & Healthcare UG courses from 2026-27** per an NCAHP notification dated 19 November 2025 — a major expansion of NEET’s footprint into physiotherapy, allied and healthcare B.Sc programmes.

## Details

### Section 1 — NEET UG Exam Fundamentals

**Conducting body:** National Testing Agency (NTA), operating under the policy framework of the National Medical Commission (NMC, nmc.org.in). Counselling is a separate function (MCC/state bodies).

**Eligibility:**

- **Age:** Minimum 17 years as on 31 December of the admission year (born on/before 31 Dec 2009 for 2026). **No upper age limit** (the earlier cap was struck down; NMC confirms no upper limit).
- **Attempts:** No cap on the number of attempts.
- **Qualifying exam:** Class 12 / equivalent with Physics, Chemistry, Biology/Biotechnology and English as core subjects. Candidates awaiting Class 12 results may sit NEET but must pass before the first counselling round.
- **Minimum PCB marks:** 50% (General/UR/EWS), 40% (OBC/SC/ST), 45% (PwD-General).
- **Nationality:** Indian nationals, NRIs, OCIs, PIOs and foreign nationals are all eligible. NRI/OCI must furnish a certificate from the Indian diplomatic mission; foreign nationals must upload proof of nationality; foreign qualifications need AIU equivalence. Admission of NRI/OCI/foreign candidates is subject to the rules of the respective state/institution.

**Exam pattern (2026, unchanged from 2025):**

- Mode: Offline, pen-and-paper, OMR-based, single session, 3 hours (180 minutes). Re-NEET 2026 was given 15 minutes extra (195 min).
- Subjects: Physics, Chemistry, Botany, Zoology — 45 questions each = 180 questions, all compulsory (the earlier optional Section B was removed in 2024/2025).
- Marks: 720 total; +4 correct, −1 wrong, 0 unattempted.
- Languages: 13 — English, Hindi, Assamese, Bengali, Gujarati, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Odia, Punjabi, Tamil, Telugu, Urdu.

**Syllabus:** NMC-notified, based on NCERT Class 11 & 12. Physics ~20 units, Chemistry ~20 units, Biology 10 units. Biology carries 360 of 720 marks (50%). Certain topics (e.g., Communication Systems, portions of Semiconductors) were removed in the 2024 revision and remain out.

**Application/timeline pattern:** Registration typically Feb–Mar (2026: 8 Feb–11 Mar), then correction window, admit card ~3–4 days before exam, exam day (strict dress code, one valid original photo ID, no last entry after gate close), provisional answer key with paid objection window, then result with AIR, category rank and percentile. Tie-breaking runs: higher Biology → higher Chemistry → higher Physics → fewer wrong answers → older candidate → random process.

### Section 2 — Full NEET UG 2026 Timeline (the Re-NEET saga)

- **8 Feb 2026:** NTA notification; original exam set for 3 May 2026; applications 8 Feb–11 Mar 2026.
- **Registration:** 22,79,743 candidates (13,32,928 female; 9,46,815 male; ~58% women)  — marginally up from 22,76,069 in 2025 (of whom 22,09,318 appeared and 12,36,531 qualified). Top registering states in 2026: Uttar Pradesh (3,56,079), Maharashtra (2,22,849), Rajasthan (2,08,583).
- **3 May 2026:** Original NEET UG conducted.
- **~12 May 2026:** Paper-leak allegation surfaces (Rajasthan SOG “guess paper” with 140 matching questions); NTA cancels the exam; CBI inquiry ordered. 
- **15 May 2026:** NTA announces re-exam via X; official notification released.
- **June 2026:** Supreme Court benches (under Justices Narasimha/Aravind Kumar; a separate plea by former ADG Health Mangala Kohli; RJD MP Sudhakar Singh’s CBT plea) decline to stop or alter the re-exam.
- **7 June 2026:** City intimation slip; **14 June 2026:** admit card.
- **21 June 2026 (Sun, 2:00–5:15 PM):** Re-NEET conducted across 5,440 centres in 551 Indian cities + 14 abroad; ~20 lakh appeared; security included central armed forces and (reportedly) IAF helicopters for paper transport.
- **25 June 2026:** Provisional answer key; objection window 25–28 June.
- **13–15 July 2026:** OMR/recorded response display; objection window closed **15 July 2026**.
- **16 July 2026:** Final answer key + result declared. 11.21 lakh qualified; joint AIR-1 at 715/720; 19 candidates scored 700+; 138 scored ≥690. (Bonus marks were reportedly awarded on a disputed question.)
- **After result:** The Supreme Court dismissed a plea challenging six answer keys in the 21 June paper.
- **Counselling:** MCC schedule released ~1 August; AIQ Round 1 from 4 August 2026.

### Section 3 — National Counselling Architecture

**The 85/15 split.** In every government medical college, 15% of seats go to the All India Quota (AIQ) pool counselled by the **Medical Counselling Committee (MCC)** under DGHS, Ministry of Health (mcc.nic.in). The remaining 85% are state-quota seats counselled by each state’s own authority.

**MCC handles:** 15% AIQ in govt colleges; **100% seats of Deemed Universities**; and central institutions — **AIIMS (all campuses), JIPMER, AMU, BHU, Jamia Millia Islamia (dentistry + internal quota), ESIC** (including 15% Insured-Persons quota), plus B.Sc Nursing at some institutions. Deemed-university seats are domicile-free, carry **no SC/ST/OBC/EWS reservation**, and include a 15% NRI quota. For 2026 MCC has renewed 1,27,028 seats and added 9,911, and increased PwD horizontal reservation in AIQ from 3% to 5%, with online document verification introduced for some (Float) candidates.

**PGIMER Chandigarh has NO MBBS** — it is a PG/super-specialty INI (MD/MS via INI-CET conducted by AIIMS Delhi). Chandigarh’s MBBS is at GMCH (AIQ via MCC, state quota by Chandigarh DME).

**AFMC Pune uses a separate hybrid process:** only the registration is routed through MCC; AFMC then shortlists ~1,700 candidates on NEET score and runs its own **ToELR (80 marks)**, Psychological Assessment Test (pass/fail), and **Interview (50 marks)** plus a medical exam. Final merit = NEET + ToELR + interview, with separate boys’/girls’ merit lists; ~150 MBBS seats (candidates must be unmarried Indian nationals).

**MCC rounds:** Round 1 → Round 2 → Round 3 (Mop-up) → Online Stray Vacancy Round, with upgradation between early rounds and “free exit” rules varying by round. Seats vacant after AIQ rounds are surrendered back to the state pool.

**AYUSH:** The **Ayush Admissions Central Counselling Committee (AACCC, aaccc.gov.in)** conducts 15% AIQ + deemed/central AYUSH seats (BAMS/BHMS/BUMS/BSMS/BNYS/B.Pharm-ITRA); 85% state AYUSH seats are counselled by state authorities. 2025 saw up to five rounds (Round 1–3 + two stray-vacancy rounds).

**On “open” seats for outsiders:** For the 85% state government pool, almost every state is effectively closed to non-domiciles. Outside-state candidates generally access other states only via (a) the 15% AIQ (MCC), (b) private/deemed college management and NRI quotas, and (c) in a few states, specific later rounds or seat categories (e.g., Madhya Pradesh from round 2, Rajasthan/Maharashtra for selected categories per that year’s rules). Deemed universities are the cleanest domicile-free route nationwide.

### Section 5 — Karnataka / KEA MBBS-BDS-AYUSH Deep Dive

**Who runs what:** KEA handles 85% state quota in government colleges + management/NRI quota in private colleges + 100% of state-quota seats in private/minority colleges. MCC runs the 15% AIQ. KEA counselled ~9,133 MBBS seats in the 2025 Round-1 pool; **Karnataka has 13,944 MBBS seats as of the 3 December 2025 NMC matrix — the highest of any state** (ahead of Uttar Pradesh 13,425, Tamil Nadu 13,050 and Maharashtra 12,824), plus roughly 3,000+ BDS seats.

**KCET–NEET linkage:** KCET/UGCET registration is **mandatory** for any candidate wanting a Karnataka state-quota MBBS/BDS/AYUSH seat — even NEET-only aspirants who never sit KCET. For 2026, KEA opened a fresh registration window 17–24 July 2026 for NEET candidates who had not registered under UGCET.

**Process flow:** Registration → document upload/verification → seat-matrix display → mock allotment → option/choice entry (locked before deadline) → provisional allotment → Round 1 → Round 2 → Mop-up/Stray Vacancy → seat acceptance/reporting. Candidates use **float vs hold** on allotted seats to control upgrades. 2026 dates: option entry to ~13 Aug, Round 1 provisional allotment ~19 Aug.

**Category/quota codes:** GM (General Merit govt seat); GMP (govt-fee seat inside a private/deemed college); OPN (open/management fee); NRI; HK & RK (Hyderabad-Karnataka/Kalyana-Karnataka Article 371(J), split into HK-region and Rest-of-Karnataka); Linguistic Minority (Tulu/Tamil/Telugu etc., needs Form 5/5A certified since school); Religious Minority; and caste/income-based reservation.

### Section 7 — Cutoff / Rank Trends

- **Qualifying (eligibility) cutoff ≠ admission (closing) cutoff.** The qualifying cutoff is a percentile threshold; the admission cutoff is the rank/marks at which seats actually close, released later by MCC/state authorities and far higher.
- **Percentile thresholds are fixed by NMC and never change:** UR/EWS 50th; OBC/SC/ST 40th; UR-PwBD 45th. Only the equivalent marks move with paper difficulty.
- **Qualifying-mark trend:** General band 720–162 (2024) → 686–144 (2025, harder paper) → 715–213 (2026, easier re-exam). OBC/SC/ST: 161–127 → 143–113 → 212–177. (PwBD lower bounds for 2026 are reported at 177 with an upper 212/194 for General-PwD; verify exact figures against the NTA notice.)
- **Admission reality (2025 AIQ):** General/OBC MBBS in government colleges closed around AIR ~26,000 in early rounds; SC/ST closed at far higher ranks (AIR ~1.36 lakh / ~1.63 lakh by Round 3). ~22,149 seats were allotted in MCC Round 1.
- **Competition:** 22,79,743 registered vs 1,29,026 MBBS seats (~63,700 government) → roughly eight qualified candidates per MBBS seat; a rank below ~50,000 (General) is broadly needed for a realistic government MBBS shot.

### Section 8 — Alternate / Adjacent NEET-Linked Courses

- **BDS (dental):** Via NEET + MCC/state (DCI-regulated). Cutoffs sit below MBBS.
- **AYUSH (BAMS/BHMS/BUMS/BSMS/BNYS/B.Pharm-ITRA):** NEET + AACCC (AIQ) or state AYUSH counselling. NCISM has introduced a 7.5-year Ayurveda variant. Government BAMS seats generally close well below MBBS; competitive states can still demand scores in the 400s for General.
- **Physiotherapy (BPT):** In Karnataka via NEET + KCET Biology route; NEET becoming a broader requirement under the allied-health reform.
- **Allied Health Sciences / B.Sc programmes:** **NEET mandatory from 2026-27** per NCAHP (communication dated 19 Nov 2025; 13 curricula notified, more coming), aligning allied health with MBBS/BDS admission standards.
- **Veterinary Science (BVSc):** 15% AIQ via NEET (VCI); state seats via state process. State pre-veterinary tests (e.g., RPVT) being merged into NEET.
- **Pharm.D / B.Pharm:** Generally a separate track; some states/institutions increasingly reference NEET.

### Section 9 — Glossary

- **NTA** — National Testing Agency (conducts NEET). **NMC** — National Medical Commission (regulator). **MCC** — Medical Counselling Committee (AIQ/deemed/central counselling). **AIQ** — All India Quota (15%). **DGHS** — Directorate General of Health Services.
- **KEA** — Karnataka Examinations Authority. **KCET/UGCET** — Karnataka Common Entrance Test. **DMER/DME/DGME/CEE/KNRUHS/Dr NTRUHS/RUHS/WBMCC/ACPUGMEC** — state counselling authorities.
- **GM/GMP/OPN/NRI** — Karnataka seat categories (general merit / govt-fee-in-private / open-management / NRI). **HK/RK & 371(J)** — Kalyana-Karnataka regional reservation. **Linguistic/Religious Minority** — minority-institution quotas.
- **EWS** — Economically Weaker Section (10%). **OBC-NCL** — OBC Non-Creamy-Layer. **PwD/PwBD** — Persons with Disability/Benchmark Disability. **Deemed University** — private institution with university status; 100% MCC, no reservation, NRI quota, high fees.
- **Float / Hold / Freeze** — option-entry choices controlling seat upgrade. **Mop-up / Stray Vacancy Round** — later counselling rounds for leftover seats. **Bond** — mandatory service/penalty commitment. **Bank Guarantee** — financial security for private-quota seats. **AACCC** — AYUSH counselling body. **NCAHP** — National Commission for Allied and Healthcare Professions. **ToELR** — AFMC’s Test of English Language, Comprehension, Logic & Reasoning.

## Recommendations

1. **Register on every relevant portal in parallel** the moment the result is out — MCC (mcc.nic.in), your state authority (KEA for Karnataka), and AACCC if AYUSH is on the table. Losing a seat because you only registered for one channel is the single most common avoidable mistake.
1. **Karnataka parents: complete UGCET/KCET registration regardless of NEET plans**, and pre-assemble the certificate stack (caste/income, HK-KK 371(J), linguistic-minority Form 5/5A, Aadhaar with exactly matching name, gap certificate for repeaters). Verification bottlenecks, not ranks, derail the most families.
1. **Map rank to a realistic seat before choice-filling.** Below ~AIR 50,000 (General), target government MBBS; in the uncertain band, fill BDS/AYUSH/GMP-private choices generously rather than gambling on a single round. Fill more choices than you think you need.
1. **Model the true cost, not the sticker fee.** For private/deemed seats add hostel/mess, bond/bank-guarantee, and the full 4.5-year tuition — for Karnataka specifically, Section 5's official 2026-27 KEA fee table puts a management/NRI-quota seat at roughly ₹1.1–2.0 crore total course cost (nationally, deemed totals run ₹85 lakh–₹1.3 crore; NRI higher). Decide the ceiling before Round 1.
1. **Benchmarks that should change your plan:** if MCC/state closing ranks in Round 1 trend tighter than last year for your category, don’t wait for a mythical late-round drop — accept and float. If a private-quota fee is revised upward after allotment (as happened at SMSIMSR in 2025), re-run the affordability math immediately before reporting.
1. **Verify every date on the official portal the day it appears** — KEA in particular extends deadlines repeatedly, and unofficial sites carry wrong dates.

## Caveats

- **Post-cutoff dates for 2026:** Some 2026 counselling dates (especially state and later MCC rounds) were still being revised as of reporting; treat any date beyond mid-August 2026 as subject to MCC/state amendment.
- **Seat-count figures vary by source and round** (1.23–1.37 lakh MBBS in some aggregators depending on whether renewals, new colleges, court-ordered and supernumerary seats are counted). The **1,29,026 across 808 colleges** figure is the NMC final matrix dated 3 December 2025; use the MCC seat matrix published before each round as the operative number.
- **Cutoff marks (715–213 etc.)** are consistently reported across education portals attributing them to NTA’s 16 July 2026 release, but the exact PwBD lower bounds should be verified against the official NTA notice; the primary PIB/NTA press note could not be opened directly.
- **Much 2026-specific reporting comes from coaching/aggregator sites**, corroborated across multiple such sources; where possible verify against nta.ac.in, nmc.org.in, mcc.nic.in and your state authority.
- **Karnataka fee and quota specifics** differ college-by-college and change annually via FRA/government orders; always cross-check the current-year KEA information bulletin.
- **Section 5's 2026-27 fee table** is transcribed from KEA's official published fee-structure document (itself sourced from two Karnataka Government Orders, MED 218 RGU 2026 and MED 213 RGU 2026, whose supporting text was in Kannada and has been summarized/translated here) — figures should match the official KEA notification, but always cross-check the source PDF or current KEA bulletin before relying on a specific college's number for a real decision, especially where this KB's summary and the underlying GO's headline figure differ slightly (e.g., Basaveshwara Medical College, Chitradurga — see Section 5's note on that).
